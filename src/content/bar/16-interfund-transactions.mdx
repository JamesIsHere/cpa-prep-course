# Interfund Transactions

## Learning Objectives

- Classify interfund activities as reciprocal or nonreciprocal and identify the four specific types
- Record interfund loans, interfund services provided and used, interfund transfers, and interfund reimbursements
- Distinguish among derived tax revenues, imposed nonexchange revenues, government-mandated nonexchange transactions, and voluntary nonexchange transactions
- Apply the correct revenue recognition timing for each category of nonexchange transaction
- Prepare journal entries for interfund transactions and understand their elimination on government-wide statements

## Types of Interfund Activities

GASB Statement No. 34 classifies interfund activities into two broad categories based on whether there is an exchange between the funds:

| Category | Nature | Types |
|----------|--------|-------|
| **Reciprocal** | Exchange-like transactions between funds | Interfund loans, Interfund services provided and used |
| **Nonreciprocal** | Non-exchange transfers and corrections | Interfund transfers, Interfund reimbursements |

Understanding this classification is critical because it determines the **account titles** used, the **financial statement presentation**, and whether the transaction is **eliminated** on the government-wide statements.

> **Exam tip:** The exam frequently tests classification of interfund activities. The key distinction is whether both funds give and receive approximately equal value (reciprocal) or whether resources move in one direction without a direct exchange (nonreciprocal). Think of reciprocal as a "business deal between funds" and nonreciprocal as a "one-way resource movement."

## Interfund Loans (Reciprocal)

An **interfund loan** occurs when one fund lends money to another fund with the expectation of repayment. This is a reciprocal transaction because the lending fund gives cash and receives a receivable; the borrowing fund receives cash and incurs a payable.

### Accounting Treatment

**Lending fund:** Records an **interfund receivable** (sometimes called "due from" or "advance to" other funds)

**Borrowing fund:** Records an **interfund payable** (sometimes called "due to" or "advance from" other funds)

Short-term interfund loans use **"Due from/Due to"** terminology. Long-term interfund loans use **"Advance to/Advance from"** terminology.

> **Exam tip:** When a governmental fund makes a long-term advance to another fund, the lending fund must report a corresponding **nonspendable fund balance** equal to the advance. This is because the resources are not available for spending — they are tied up in the long-term receivable.

**Worked Example — Interfund Loan:**

The General Fund lends $200,000 to the Capital Projects Fund, to be repaid in 3 years.

General Fund entries:
- Dr. Advance to Capital Projects Fund — $200,000
- Cr. Cash — $200,000
- Note: Nonspendable fund balance increases by $200,000

Capital Projects Fund entries:
- Dr. Cash — $200,000
- Cr. Advance from General Fund — $200,000

**Government-wide elimination:** Interfund loans between governmental funds are eliminated because both funds are part of governmental activities. The advance receivable and payable net to zero.

### Short-Term vs. Long-Term Distinction

| Feature | Short-Term (Due from/to) | Long-Term (Advance to/from) |
|---------|--------------------------|----------------------------|
| Expected repayment | Within one year | Beyond one year |
| Lending fund impact | Current asset | Noncurrent asset + nonspendable fund balance |
| Borrowing fund impact | Current liability | Long-term liability (not in fund statements) |

## Interfund Services Provided and Used (Reciprocal)

**Interfund services provided and used** occur when one fund charges another fund for goods or services at approximate fair market value. This mirrors an arm's-length transaction.

### Examples

- The Internal Service Fund charges the General Fund for fleet maintenance services
- The Water Enterprise Fund charges the General Fund for water used in city parks
- The General Fund charges the Special Revenue Fund for centralized payroll processing

### Accounting Treatment

These transactions are recorded as **revenues** in the providing fund and **expenditures or expenses** in the receiving fund — the same as they would be for transactions with external parties.

**Worked Example — Interfund Services:**

The city's Internal Service Fund (Fleet Maintenance) bills the General Fund $85,000 for vehicle repairs during the year.

Internal Service Fund:
- Dr. Due from General Fund — $85,000
- Cr. Charges for services revenue — $85,000

General Fund:
- Dr. Vehicle maintenance expenditure — $85,000
- Cr. Due to Internal Service Fund — $85,000

**Government-wide treatment:** Interfund services between governmental activities are generally **not eliminated** because they represent legitimate operating costs. However, the "due from/due to" balances are eliminated on the statement of net position.

> **Exam tip:** Interfund services are the only interfund activity where the transaction is treated like an external transaction in the providing fund's revenues. All other interfund activities use special account titles (transfers, due from/to). If the exam describes a fund charging another fund at or near fair market value for services, classify it as interfund services provided and used.

## Interfund Transfers (Nonreciprocal)

An **interfund transfer** is a flow of resources from one fund to another without a direct exchange. The most common examples are transfers from the General Fund to other funds to finance their operations or projects.

### Accounting Treatment

**Sending fund:** Records a **transfer out** (reported as "other financing uses" in governmental funds)

**Receiving fund:** Records a **transfer in** (reported as "other financing sources" in governmental funds)

Transfers are reported **after** revenues, expenditures, and the excess/deficiency line on the governmental fund operating statement. They are **not** revenues or expenditures.

**Worked Example — Interfund Transfer:**

The General Fund transfers $1,500,000 to the Debt Service Fund to cover annual debt service requirements.

General Fund:
- Dr. Transfer out to Debt Service Fund — $1,500,000
- Cr. Cash — $1,500,000

Debt Service Fund:
- Dr. Cash — $1,500,000
- Cr. Transfer in from General Fund — $1,500,000

**Government-wide elimination:** Transfers between governmental funds are **fully eliminated** because they are internal movements within governmental activities. Transfers between governmental and business-type activities are **not eliminated** but are reported as transfers on the government-wide statement of activities.

### Financial Statement Presentation

On the governmental fund statement of revenues, expenditures, and changes in fund balances:

| Line Item | Amount |
|-----------|--------|
| Total revenues | $XX,XXX |
| Total expenditures | (XX,XXX) |
| **Excess (deficiency) of revenues over expenditures** | $X,XXX |
| Other financing sources: Transfer in | X,XXX |
| Other financing uses: Transfer out | (X,XXX) |
| **Net change in fund balance** | $X,XXX |

## Interfund Reimbursements (Nonreciprocal)

An **interfund reimbursement** occurs when one fund initially pays for an expenditure that properly belongs to another fund. The reimbursement corrects the original recording, moving the expenditure to the proper fund.

### Accounting Treatment

The fund being reimbursed **reduces** its expenditure (as if the transaction never occurred). The fund making the reimbursement **records** the expenditure.

**Worked Example — Interfund Reimbursement:**

The General Fund inadvertently pays $12,000 for supplies that should have been charged to the Special Revenue Fund.

When the reimbursement is received:

General Fund:
- Dr. Cash — $12,000
- Cr. Supplies expenditure — $12,000 (reverses the original charge)

Special Revenue Fund:
- Dr. Supplies expenditure — $12,000
- Cr. Cash — $12,000

> **Exam tip:** Interfund reimbursements are unique because they do **not** appear as separate line items on the financial statements. They simply correct an error in the initial recording. Think of them as reclassifications, not transfers. The exam may try to trick you by including a reimbursement among the transfer amounts.

## Nonexchange Revenue Transactions

Governmental entities derive much of their revenue from **nonexchange transactions** — transactions in which the government receives value without directly giving equal value in exchange. GASB Statement No. 33 classifies these into four categories with distinct recognition rules.

### Category 1: Derived Tax Revenues

**Derived tax revenues** are taxes imposed on exchange transactions. The government derives revenue from an underlying transaction between other parties.

| Tax Type | Underlying Exchange | Recognition |
|----------|-------------------|-------------|
| Sales tax | Buyer-seller transaction | When the underlying transaction occurs (or when resources are received, if earlier) |
| Income tax | Employer-employee compensation | When the underlying transaction occurs |
| Motor fuel tax | Purchase of gasoline | When the underlying transaction occurs |

**Modified accrual adjustment:** Revenue is recognized only if collected within the availability period (typically 60 days after year-end). Amounts not yet available are reported as deferred inflows.

### Category 2: Imposed Nonexchange Revenues

**Imposed nonexchange revenues** result from assessments by the government on nongovernmental entities, including individuals and organizations. Unlike derived tax revenues, there is no underlying exchange transaction.

| Revenue Type | Recognition | Key Rule |
|-------------|-------------|----------|
| Property taxes | Revenue in the **period for which levied** (not when cash is received) | Must be recognized net of estimated uncollectibles |
| Fines and penalties | When imposed or when resources are received | One-time assessments |
| Special assessments | Over the period to which they relate | May span multiple years |

**Worked Example — Property Tax Revenue:**

In November Year 1, a city levies $10,000,000 in property taxes for Year 2. By Year 2 year-end, $9,200,000 has been collected. Another $500,000 is expected to be collected within 60 days. Estimated uncollectibles are $200,000.

Fund-level revenue recognized in Year 2: $9,200,000 + $500,000 = **$9,700,000** (collected plus available within 60 days). The remaining $100,000 ($10,000,000 - $200,000 uncollectible - $9,700,000) is a deferred inflow.

Government-wide revenue: $10,000,000 - $200,000 = **$9,800,000** (full accrual recognizes when levied, net of uncollectibles).

### Category 3: Government-Mandated Nonexchange Transactions

These occur when a **higher level of government** requires a lower level to perform an activity and provides funding. The providing government mandates the activity.

| Example | Provider | Recipient | Recognition |
|---------|----------|-----------|-------------|
| Federal education grants with requirements | Federal government | State/local school districts | When eligibility requirements are met |
| State-mandated pollution cleanup with funding | State government | Local governments | When eligibility requirements are met |

Revenue recognition occurs when all **eligibility requirements** are met, including:
- Required characteristics of recipients
- Time requirements (the period to which the grant applies)
- Reimbursement requirements (qualifying expenditures incurred)
- Contingencies (specific actions taken)

### Category 4: Voluntary Nonexchange Transactions

These result from **voluntary** agreements between parties. The providing government or organization is not compelled by a higher authority to make the grant.

| Example | Key Feature | Recognition |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| Federal grants without mandates | Voluntary, with eligibility requirements | When eligibility requirements are met |
| Private donations to government | Voluntary, may have purpose restrictions | When eligibility requirements are met |
| Certain entitlement programs | Voluntary participation | When eligibility requirements are met |

**Worked Example — Government-Mandated Grant:**

The state provides a $2,000,000 education grant to Riverview County, requiring the county to spend the funds on approved educational programs and match 10% with local funds. The grant covers the fiscal year ending June 30.

Recognition: Revenue is recognized when the county meets **all** eligibility requirements — the time period has arrived (current fiscal year), the county has incurred qualifying expenditures, and the matching requirement is met. Under modified accrual, the cash must also be available.

> **Exam tip:** For Categories 3 and 4 (government-mandated and voluntary), always check for eligibility requirements. Revenue cannot be recognized until ALL requirements are satisfied. This is a common trap: the exam may describe a grant with a time requirement where the eligibility period has not yet arrived.

## Summary: Elimination on Government-Wide Statements

| Transaction Type | Between Governmental Funds | Between Governmental and Business-Type |
|-----------------|---------------------------|---------------------------------------|
| Interfund loans | Eliminated | Reported as internal balances |
| Interfund services | Not eliminated (legitimate cost) | Not eliminated |
| Interfund transfers | Eliminated | Reported as transfers line item |
| Interfund reimbursements | Not separately reported | Not separately reported |

## Key Terms

- **Reciprocal interfund activity** — Exchange-like transactions between funds where both parties give and receive approximately equal value, including interfund loans and interfund services provided and used
- **Nonreciprocal interfund activity** — One-directional resource flows between funds without a direct exchange, including interfund transfers and interfund reimbursements
- **Interfund loan** — A lending arrangement between funds; short-term loans use "due from/to" and long-term loans use "advance to/from"
- **Interfund services provided and used** — Charges between funds at or near fair market value for goods or services; recorded as revenue and expenditure/expense
- **Interfund transfer** — A flow of resources from one fund to another without a direct exchange; reported as other financing sources/uses
- **Interfund reimbursement** — A repayment by one fund to another for expenditures initially recorded in the wrong fund; not separately reported
- **Derived tax revenue** — Revenue from taxes imposed on underlying exchange transactions (sales tax, income tax)
- **Imposed nonexchange revenue** — Revenue from government assessments with no underlying exchange (property taxes, fines)
- **Government-mandated nonexchange transaction** — A higher-level government requires a program and provides funding; recognized when eligibility requirements are met
- **Voluntary nonexchange transaction** — A voluntary agreement to provide resources; recognized when eligibility requirements are met
- **Availability criterion** — Under modified accrual, revenue must be collected within the availability period (typically 60 days) to be recognized
- **Nonspendable fund balance** — Fund balance component that includes amounts not in spendable form, such as long-term advances to other funds
