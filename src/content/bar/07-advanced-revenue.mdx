# Advanced Revenue and Intangibles

## Learning Objectives

- Apply ASC 606 to complex multi-element arrangements and variable consideration
- Account for contract modifications, licensing, and principal-vs-agent arrangements
- Apply the impairment models for goodwill (ASC 350) and long-lived assets (ASC 360)
- Distinguish between internally developed and acquired intangible assets

## ASC 606: Complex Revenue Recognition

The five-step model from ASC 606 applies to all contracts with customers:

1. **Identify the contract** with the customer
2. **Identify the performance obligations** in the contract
3. **Determine the transaction price**
4. **Allocate the transaction price** to performance obligations
5. **Recognize revenue** when (or as) each obligation is satisfied

BAR goes deeper into complex applications of this framework.

### Variable Consideration

Variable consideration includes discounts, rebates, refunds, credits, price concessions, incentives, performance bonuses, and penalties. ASC 606 requires the entity to **estimate** variable consideration using one of two methods:

- **Expected value** — Probability-weighted sum of possible outcomes (best when many possible outcomes)
- **Most likely amount** — Single most likely outcome (best for binary outcomes — yes/no, pass/fail)

The estimate is subject to the **constraint**: include variable consideration only to the extent it is probable that a significant reversal will not occur when uncertainty resolves.

**Worked Example:**

A construction company has a $10M contract with a $500K bonus if completed by March 31. Management estimates a 70% probability of on-time completion.

- Expected value: 70% × $500,000 + 30% × $0 = $350,000
- Most likely amount: $500,000 (since 70% > 50%, the most likely outcome is receiving the bonus)

The company would use whichever method better predicts the outcome. If the constraint is met (no probable significant reversal), include the estimate in the transaction price.

### Allocating Transaction Price

When a contract has **multiple performance obligations**, allocate the total transaction price based on **relative standalone selling prices (SSP)**:

Allocation to obligation = (SSP of obligation / Total SSP of all obligations) × Transaction price

If standalone selling price is not directly observable, estimate using:

1. **Adjusted market assessment** — What the market would pay
2. **Expected cost plus margin** — Cost to satisfy plus a reasonable margin
3. **Residual approach** — Total price minus observable SSPs (allowed only when SSP is highly variable or uncertain)

**Worked Example:**

A software company sells a bundle for $180,000: License (SSP $120,000), Implementation (SSP $40,000), One year of support (SSP $40,000). Total SSP = $200,000.

- License: ($120,000 / $200,000) × $180,000 = **$108,000**
- Implementation: ($40,000 / $200,000) × $180,000 = **$36,000**
- Support: ($40,000 / $200,000) × $180,000 = **$36,000**

### Contract Modifications

A contract modification is a change in scope, price, or both. Accounting depends on whether the modification creates a **separate contract**:

**Separate contract** if both: (1) distinct goods/services are added AND (2) the price increases by an amount that reflects standalone selling price. Account prospectively — like a new contract.

**Not a separate contract** — Account as either:

- **Prospective adjustment** — If remaining goods/services are distinct from those already transferred. Adjust the transaction price and allocate to remaining obligations going forward.
- **Cumulative catch-up** — If remaining goods/services are NOT distinct (part of a single performance obligation partially satisfied). Adjust revenue recognized to date.

### Licensing

The nature of the license determines the revenue pattern:

- **Right to use** (functional IP) — Revenue at a **point in time** when the license transfers. The customer can use the IP independently (e.g., software, film, music).
- **Right to access** (symbolic IP) — Revenue **over time** as the customer accesses the IP. The entity's ongoing activities significantly affect the IP (e.g., brand name, franchise, team logo).

> **Exam tip:** Software licenses are almost always "right to use" (point-in-time). Franchise rights are almost always "right to access" (over time). The key question is: does the licensor's ongoing activity change the value of the IP?

### Principal vs. Agent

When a third party is involved in providing goods or services, determine whether the entity is the **principal** (controls the good/service before transfer) or the **agent** (arranges for another party to provide it):

| Factor | Principal | Agent |
|--------|-----------|-------|
| Revenue reported | Gross (full amount) | Net (commission only) |
| Controls the good/service? | Yes | No |
| Bears inventory risk? | Yes | No |
| Sets the price? | Yes | No |

**Worked Example:**

An online marketplace charges sellers a 15% commission. A product sells for $100. The marketplace never takes possession.

- Marketplace is the **agent**: Reports revenue of **$15** (commission)
- If the marketplace took title and inventory risk: Reports revenue of **$100** (principal)

## Goodwill Impairment (ASC 350)

Goodwill is tested for impairment **annually** at the reporting unit level, or more frequently if triggering events occur.

### Quantitative Test

Compare the **fair value of the reporting unit** to its **carrying amount** (including goodwill):

- If fair value ≥ carrying amount: **No impairment**
- If fair value < carrying amount: Impairment loss = Carrying amount - Fair value (but not to exceed the carrying amount of goodwill)

### Qualitative Assessment (Step 0)

ASC 350-20 allows a **qualitative screen** first: assess whether it is "more likely than not" (>50%) that fair value is less than carrying amount. If not, skip the quantitative test.

Qualitative factors: macroeconomic conditions, industry trends, cost factors, financial performance, entity-specific events, share price changes.

> **Exam tip:** Goodwill impairment is a one-way street — once written down, goodwill can never be written back up under GAAP. Also, goodwill is not amortized for public companies (indefinite-life intangible).

## Long-Lived Asset Impairment (ASC 360)

Long-lived assets (PP&E, finite-life intangibles) use a **two-step impairment test** when triggering events occur:

**Step 1 — Recoverability test:** Is the carrying amount recoverable from **undiscounted future cash flows**? If undiscounted cash flows ≥ carrying amount, no impairment.

**Step 2 — Measurement:** If not recoverable, impairment loss = Carrying amount - **Fair value**.

| Feature | Goodwill (ASC 350) | Long-Lived Assets (ASC 360) |
|---------|--------------------|-----------------------------|
| Trigger | Annual + events | Events only (no annual test) |
| Step 1 test | FV vs. carrying | Undiscounted cash flows vs. carrying |
| Measurement | FV vs. carrying | FV vs. carrying |
| Reversal | Never | Never |

## Key Terms

- **Variable consideration** — Components of the transaction price that are uncertain at contract inception, estimated using expected value or most likely amount methods
- **Standalone selling price** — The price at which an entity would sell a promised good or service separately; used to allocate the transaction price in multi-element arrangements
- **Goodwill impairment** — A non-reversible write-down of goodwill when the fair value of a reporting unit falls below its carrying amount
- **Functional IP** — Intellectual property with significant standalone functionality, recognized at a point in time (right to use)

## Practice Problems

**1.** A software company sells a bundle for $90,000 containing a license (SSP $60,000) and 2 years of support (SSP $40,000). How much revenue is allocated to the license?

a) $60,000
b) $54,000
c) $45,000
d) $50,000

**Answer:** b) $54,000 — Total SSP = $100,000. License allocation = ($60,000 / $100,000) × $90,000 = $54,000.

---

**2.** A reporting unit has a carrying amount of $5,000,000 (including goodwill of $1,200,000) and a fair value of $4,200,000. What is the goodwill impairment loss?

a) $1,200,000
b) $800,000
c) $0
d) $2,000,000

**Answer:** b) $800,000 — Impairment = Carrying amount - Fair value = $5,000,000 - $4,200,000 = $800,000. Since $800,000 is less than the goodwill carrying amount of $1,200,000, the full $800,000 is recorded as an impairment loss.

---

**3.** An online travel agency books hotel rooms for customers. The agency does not take possession of the rooms, does not set room prices, and earns a 12% commission. How should the agency report revenue on a $200 booking?

a) $200 gross revenue
b) $24 net revenue
c) $176 net revenue
d) $200 gross revenue with $176 cost of sales

**Answer:** b) $24 net revenue — The agency is an agent because it does not control the service before transfer. Revenue is reported net: $200 × 12% = $24.
