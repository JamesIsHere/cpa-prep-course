# Financial Statement Analysis

## Learning Objectives

- Calculate and interpret liquidity, solvency, profitability, and activity ratios
- Perform horizontal and vertical analysis on financial statements
- Apply DuPont analysis to decompose return on equity
- Evaluate financial health using trend analysis and benchmarking

## Ratio Analysis Framework

Financial statement analysis converts raw financial data into meaningful measures for evaluating a company's performance, position, and trends. Ratios fall into four categories: **liquidity**, **solvency**, **profitability**, and **activity**.

### Liquidity Ratios

Liquidity ratios measure a company's ability to meet **short-term obligations** as they come due.

| Ratio | Formula | Interpretation |
|-------|---------|----------------|
| Current ratio | Current assets / Current liabilities | Above 1.0 indicates short-term solvency |
| Quick ratio | (Cash + Short-term investments + Receivables) / Current liabilities | Excludes inventory and prepaids |
| Cash ratio | Cash and equivalents / Current liabilities | Most conservative liquidity measure |

> **Exam tip:** The quick ratio is sometimes called the "acid-test ratio." Remember it strips out inventory because inventory may not be quickly convertible to cash, especially for manufacturers.

**Worked Example:**

A company reports: Cash $40,000; Receivables $80,000; Inventory $60,000; Prepaid expenses $10,000; Current liabilities $100,000.

- Current ratio: ($40,000 + $80,000 + $60,000 + $10,000) / $100,000 = **1.90**
- Quick ratio: ($40,000 + $80,000) / $100,000 = **1.20**
- Cash ratio: $40,000 / $100,000 = **0.40**

### Solvency Ratios

Solvency ratios measure a company's ability to meet **long-term obligations** and assess capital structure risk.

| Ratio | Formula | Interpretation |
|-------|---------|----------------|
| Debt-to-equity | Total liabilities / Total equity | Higher = more leverage |
| Debt ratio | Total liabilities / Total assets | Percentage of assets financed by debt |
| Times interest earned | EBIT / Interest expense | Ability to cover interest payments |
| Equity multiplier | Total assets / Total equity | Leverage component of DuPont |

### Profitability Ratios

Profitability ratios measure a company's ability to generate **earnings relative to revenue, assets, or equity**.

| Ratio | Formula | Interpretation |
|-------|---------|----------------|
| Gross profit margin | (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue | Pricing and production efficiency |
| Operating profit margin | Operating income / Revenue | Core business profitability |
| Net profit margin | Net income / Revenue | Bottom-line profitability |
| Return on assets (ROA) | Net income / Average total assets | Asset utilization efficiency |
| Return on equity (ROE) | Net income / Average equity | Return to shareholders |

### Activity Ratios

Activity ratios measure how efficiently a company manages its **operating assets**.

| Ratio | Formula | Interpretation |
|-------|---------|----------------|
| Inventory turnover | COGS / Average inventory | Times inventory sells per year |
| Days sales in inventory | 365 / Inventory turnover | Days to sell inventory |
| Receivables turnover | Net credit sales / Average receivables | Times receivables collected per year |
| Days sales outstanding | 365 / Receivables turnover | Average collection period |
| Asset turnover | Revenue / Average total assets | Revenue per dollar of assets |
| Payables turnover | COGS / Average payables | Times payables are paid per year |

> **Exam tip:** The operating cycle = Days sales in inventory + Days sales outstanding. The cash conversion cycle subtracts days payable outstanding: Operating cycle - Days payable outstanding.

## DuPont Analysis

DuPont analysis decomposes **ROE** into three drivers, revealing whether returns come from profitability, efficiency, or leverage:

**ROE = Net profit margin x Asset turnover x Equity multiplier**

Or equivalently:

**ROE = (Net income / Revenue) x (Revenue / Average assets) x (Average assets / Average equity)**

**Worked Example:**

Company XYZ reports: Net income $120,000; Revenue $2,000,000; Average total assets $1,500,000; Average equity $600,000.

- Net profit margin: $120,000 / $2,000,000 = **6.0%**
- Asset turnover: $2,000,000 / $1,500,000 = **1.33x**
- Equity multiplier: $1,500,000 / $600,000 = **2.50x**
- ROE: 6.0% x 1.33 x 2.50 = **20.0%** (verify: $120,000 / $600,000 = 20.0%)

DuPont tells us this company's 20% ROE is driven more by leverage (2.50x) than by margins (6.0%). A competitor with 10% margins, 1.5x turnover, and 1.3x multiplier has ROE of 19.5% — similar returns with much less risk.

## Horizontal and Vertical Analysis

### Horizontal Analysis (Trend Analysis)

Compares financial data **across multiple periods** to identify trends.

- Dollar change = Current year - Base year
- Percentage change = (Current year - Base year) / Base year x 100

### Vertical Analysis (Common-Size Statements)

Expresses each line item as a **percentage of a base amount** within a single period:

- Income statement: each item as a % of revenue
- Balance sheet: each item as a % of total assets

**Worked Example — Common-Size Income Statement:**

| Line Item | Amount | % of Revenue |
|-----------|--------|-------------|
| Revenue | $500,000 | 100.0% |
| COGS | $300,000 | 60.0% |
| Gross profit | $200,000 | 40.0% |
| SG&A | $100,000 | 20.0% |
| Operating income | $100,000 | 20.0% |
| Interest expense | $20,000 | 4.0% |
| Tax expense | $24,000 | 4.8% |
| Net income | $56,000 | 11.2% |

Vertical analysis makes it easy to compare companies of different sizes — a $5M company and a $500M company can both be compared on the basis of margin percentages.

## Benchmarking

Ratios are most meaningful when compared against:

1. **Prior periods** — Is the company improving or deteriorating?
2. **Industry averages** — How does the company compare to peers?
3. **Competitor companies** — Direct head-to-head comparison
4. **Management targets** — Is the company meeting internal goals?

A declining current ratio in isolation might seem concerning, but if the industry average dropped similarly due to supply chain changes, it may be normal.

## Key Terms

- **DuPont analysis** — Decomposes ROE into profit margin, asset turnover, and equity multiplier to identify the drivers of shareholder returns
- **Common-size statement** — Financial statement where each line is expressed as a percentage of a base amount (revenue for income statement, total assets for balance sheet)
- **Operating cycle** — Days inventory outstanding plus days sales outstanding; measures how long it takes to convert inventory into cash
- **Cash conversion cycle** — Operating cycle minus days payable outstanding; measures the net time cash is tied up in operations

## Practice Problems

**1.** A company reports current assets of $240,000 (including inventory of $90,000 and prepaid expenses of $15,000) and current liabilities of $150,000. What is the quick ratio?

a) 1.60
b) 1.00
c) 0.90
d) 1.50

**Answer:** c) 0.90 — Quick ratio = (Cash + Short-term investments + Receivables) / Current liabilities = ($240,000 - $90,000 - $15,000) / $150,000 = $135,000 / $150,000 = 0.90.

---

**2.** Using DuPont analysis, a company has a net profit margin of 8%, asset turnover of 1.5, and an equity multiplier of 2.0. What is the return on equity?

a) 12%
b) 16%
c) 24%
d) 11.5%

**Answer:** c) 24% — ROE = Net profit margin x Asset turnover x Equity multiplier = 8% x 1.5 x 2.0 = 24%.

---

**3.** A company's COGS is $800,000 and average inventory is $160,000. How many days does it take to sell inventory on average?

a) 5 days
b) 73 days
c) 50 days
d) 200 days

**Answer:** b) 73 days — Inventory turnover = $800,000 / $160,000 = 5.0. Days sales in inventory = 365 / 5.0 = 73 days.
