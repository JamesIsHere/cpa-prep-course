# Stock Compensation and Business Combinations

## Learning Objectives

- Account for stock options, restricted stock, and performance shares under ASC 718
- Apply the acquisition method for business combinations under ASC 805
- Calculate goodwill in a business combination and identify measurement period adjustments
- Account for contingent consideration and bargain purchases

## Stock-Based Compensation (ASC 718)

ASC 718 requires companies to recognize **compensation expense** for all share-based payment arrangements at **fair value** on the **grant date**.

### Stock Options

A stock option gives the employee the right to purchase shares at a fixed **exercise price** after a **vesting period**.

**Measurement:** Fair value is determined at the grant date using an option pricing model (Black-Scholes or binomial lattice). Once measured, the fair value is **not adjusted** for subsequent stock price changes.

**Recognition:** Expense is recognized **over the requisite service period** (usually the vesting period), typically using straight-line allocation.

**Worked Example:**

On January 1, Year 1, a company grants 10,000 stock options with a fair value of $8 each. The vesting period is 4 years.

Total compensation cost: 10,000 × $8 = $80,000

Annual expense (straight-line): $80,000 / 4 = **$20,000 per year**

Journal entry each year:
- Debit: Compensation expense $20,000
- Credit: Additional paid-in capital (APIC — stock options) $20,000

When options are exercised (assume exercise price $25 per share, all 10,000 exercised):
- Debit: Cash (10,000 × $25) = $250,000
- Debit: APIC — stock options $80,000
- Credit: Common stock + APIC $330,000

> **Exam tip:** If options expire unexercised, the APIC — stock options balance is reclassified to APIC — expired options. No expense is reversed (the compensation already happened during the service period).

### Restricted Stock

Restricted stock is **actual shares** issued to the employee, subject to forfeiture if conditions are not met.

**Measurement:** Fair value = Market price on the grant date (no option pricing model needed since there is no exercise price).

**Recognition:** Same as options — over the vesting period.

**Worked Example:**

A company grants 5,000 restricted shares when the stock price is $30. Vesting period: 3 years.

Total cost: 5,000 × $30 = $150,000
Annual expense: $150,000 / 3 = **$50,000 per year**

### Forfeitures

ASC 718 allows two approaches:

1. **Estimate forfeitures at grant date** — Reduce total expense by expected forfeitures; adjust if actual forfeitures differ
2. **Recognize forfeitures as they occur** (policy election) — Record full expense, then reverse if employees leave before vesting

### Modifications

If the terms of an award are modified, the entity recognizes at minimum the **original grant-date fair value**. Any incremental fair value (new FV minus old FV at modification date) is recognized over the remaining vesting period.

## Business Combinations (ASC 805)

A **business combination** occurs when an acquirer obtains control of one or more businesses. ASC 805 requires the **acquisition method**.

### Acquisition Method Steps

1. **Identify the acquirer** — The entity that obtains control
2. **Determine the acquisition date** — Date control transfers (usually closing date)
3. **Recognize and measure identifiable assets, liabilities, and NCI** — At fair value on the acquisition date
4. **Recognize goodwill or a bargain purchase gain**

### Calculating Goodwill

Goodwill = Consideration transferred + NCI + Previously held equity interest - Fair value of net identifiable assets

**Worked Example:**

Company A acquires 100% of Company B for $5,000,000 cash. On the acquisition date:

| Item | Book Value | Fair Value |
|------|-----------|------------|
| Current assets | $800,000 | $800,000 |
| Property & equipment | $2,000,000 | $2,500,000 |
| Customer relationships | $0 | $400,000 |
| Current liabilities | ($300,000) | ($300,000) |
| Long-term debt | ($1,000,000) | ($1,050,000) |
| **Net identifiable assets** | **$1,500,000** | **$2,350,000** |

Goodwill = $5,000,000 - $2,350,000 = **$2,650,000**

Note: Customer relationships ($400,000) are recognized as an intangible asset even though they were not on Company B's books. ASC 805 requires recognition of identifiable intangible assets at fair value.

> **Exam tip:** Identifiable intangible assets that must be separately recognized in a business combination include: customer lists, trade names, patents, technology, non-compete agreements, and order backlogs. These are recognized even if they were internally developed by the acquiree.

### Contingent Consideration

The acquirer may agree to make additional payments based on future events (earn-outs). Under ASC 805:

- Recognize at **fair value** on the acquisition date (included in goodwill calculation)
- Classify as a **liability** or **equity**
- If liability: remeasure at fair value each period — changes go to **earnings** (not goodwill)
- If equity: no remeasurement after initial recognition

### Bargain Purchase (Negative Goodwill)

If the fair value of net identifiable assets exceeds the consideration paid:

1. Re-examine all values to ensure accuracy
2. If the excess remains after re-examination, recognize a **gain on bargain purchase** in earnings on the acquisition date

### Measurement Period

The acquirer has up to **12 months** from the acquisition date to finalize the fair values of assets and liabilities. Adjustments during the measurement period are recorded **retroactively** (as if they were known at the acquisition date).

### Acquisition Costs

Direct acquisition costs (legal fees, due diligence, finder's fees) are **expensed as incurred** — they are NOT included in the purchase price or added to goodwill. This is a common exam trap.

The only exception: costs of issuing debt or equity securities follow their respective standards (debt issuance costs amortized; equity issuance costs reduce APIC).

## Noncontrolling Interests (NCI)

When less than 100% of a subsidiary is acquired, the acquirer must recognize the **noncontrolling interest** at fair value on the acquisition date.

NCI is reported in the **equity section** of the consolidated balance sheet, separate from the parent's equity. NCI's share of subsidiary net income is reported on the consolidated income statement.

## Key Terms

- **Grant-date fair value** — The value of a stock-based award measured once at the date of grant, not subsequently adjusted for stock price changes
- **Goodwill** — The excess of acquisition consideration over the fair value of identifiable net assets acquired in a business combination
- **Contingent consideration** — Additional purchase price payments contingent on future events; classified as liability (remeasured) or equity (not remeasured)
- **Measurement period** — Up to 12 months after a business combination during which the acquirer may adjust provisional fair values retroactively
