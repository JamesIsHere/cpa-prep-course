# State and Local Government Reporting

## Learning Objectives

- Apply the GASB reporting model including government-wide and fund-level statements
- Distinguish between governmental, proprietary, and fiduciary fund types
- Account for capital assets, infrastructure, and long-term obligations in governmental entities
- Compare GASB and FASB frameworks for key accounting differences

## GASB Reporting Model (GASB 34)

State and local governments prepare **two sets** of financial statements:

1. **Government-wide statements** — Full accrual basis, economic resources focus (similar to commercial entities)
2. **Fund financial statements** — Modified accrual for governmental funds, full accrual for proprietary and fiduciary funds

### Government-Wide Statements

| Statement | What It Shows |
|-----------|--------------|
| Statement of Net Position | Assets + deferred outflows - Liabilities - deferred inflows = Net position |
| Statement of Activities | Expenses by function, program revenues, general revenues, change in net position |

The statement of activities uses a **net (expense) format**: each function's expenses are offset by program revenues (charges for services, operating grants, capital grants) to show the net cost. General revenues (taxes, unrestricted grants) are reported separately.

> **Exam tip:** Government-wide statements report only governmental and business-type activities. Fiduciary activities are excluded because those resources are not available to support the government's own programs.

### Fund Financial Statements

Three fund categories:

**Governmental funds** (modified accrual, current financial resources):
- **General fund** — Main operating fund; always a major fund
- **Special revenue funds** — Legally restricted for specific purposes
- **Capital projects funds** — Resources for acquiring or constructing major capital assets
- **Debt service funds** — Resources for paying principal and interest on general long-term debt
- **Permanent funds** — Resources where principal must be preserved (only earnings are spendable)

**Proprietary funds** (full accrual, economic resources):
- **Enterprise funds** — Business-type activities (water utility, airport, parking garage)
- **Internal service funds** — Services provided to other government departments (fleet maintenance, IT)

**Fiduciary funds** (full accrual, reported separately):
- **Pension trust funds** — Employee retirement systems
- **Investment trust funds** — External investment pools
- **Private-purpose trust funds** — Resources held for external individuals/organizations
- **Custodial funds** — Resources held temporarily for others (formerly agency funds)

## Modified Accrual Accounting

Governmental funds use **modified accrual** — revenues are recognized when **measurable and available** (collectible within the current period or soon enough to pay current liabilities, typically 60 days). Expenditures (not expenses) are recognized when the fund liability is incurred, except for:

- **Debt service** — Principal and interest recognized when **due** (payment date)
- **Compensated absences, claims, pensions** — Recognized only to the extent they will be paid with **current financial resources**

### Revenue Recognition

Property taxes are recognized in the period for which they are **levied**, provided they are collected within the availability period. Estimate and report an **allowance for uncollectible taxes**.

Derived tax revenues (income taxes, sales taxes) — Recognized when the underlying transaction occurs, subject to the availability criterion.

Government-mandated and voluntary nonexchange transactions (grants) — Recognized when all **eligibility requirements** are met and resources are available.

**Worked Example:**

A city levies $5,000,000 in property taxes for the fiscal year ending June 30. It expects to collect $4,800,000 (96%). Of the total, $4,600,000 is collected by June 30 and $150,000 is collected in July-August (within 60 days).

Revenue recognized: $4,600,000 + $150,000 = **$4,750,000**
Deferred inflows (collected after availability period): $50,000
Allowance for uncollectible: $200,000

## Capital Assets and Infrastructure

### Government-Wide Reporting

Capital assets (including infrastructure such as roads, bridges, and water systems) are reported on the government-wide statement of net position and **depreciated** over their useful lives.

**Modified approach** (alternative for infrastructure): Instead of depreciating eligible infrastructure assets, a government may:
1. Manage the assets using an **asset management system**
2. Maintain them at a condition level established by the government
3. Report maintenance costs as expenses instead of depreciation

### Fund-Level Reporting

Governmental funds do **not** report capital assets or depreciation. Capital outlays are recorded as **expenditures** in the period of purchase. The reconciliation between fund statements and government-wide statements adds back capital assets and removes expenditures for capital outlays.

## Long-Term Obligations

### Government-Wide

Long-term debt (bonds, notes, capital leases, pensions, OPEB) is reported as a liability on the government-wide statement of net position.

### Fund-Level

Governmental funds report long-term debt **only when currently due** — the proceeds from bond issuance are reported as an **other financing source**, and principal payments are expenditures when due. The reconciliation adds back the long-term portion.

Bond premiums and discounts: In governmental funds, bond premium is reported as an other financing source. In government-wide statements, premium is amortized over the life of the bonds using the effective interest method.

## Pensions (GASB 68) and OPEB (GASB 75)

### Net Pension Liability

The government-wide statements report a **net pension liability** (or asset):

Net pension liability = Total pension liability - Plan net position (fair value of plan assets)

Pension expense includes: service cost, interest on TPL, changes in benefit terms, minus expected return on plan assets, plus amortization of deferred items.

**Deferred outflows/inflows** arise from:
- Differences between expected and actual experience
- Changes in actuarial assumptions
- Differences between projected and actual investment earnings
- Employer contributions made after the measurement date

> **Exam tip:** GASB 68 requires governments to report the net pension liability on the face of the government-wide statements — not just in the notes. This was a major change from prior standards where the liability was often disclosed but not recognized.

### OPEB (GASB 75)

Other post-employment benefits (retiree healthcare, life insurance) follow the same framework as pensions:
- Report **net OPEB liability** on government-wide statements
- Same deferred outflow/inflow categories
- Service cost, interest, and amortization components of expense

## GASB vs. FASB Key Differences

| Topic | GASB | FASB |
|-------|------|------|
| Reporting model | Government-wide + fund statements | Single set of financial statements |
| Measurement focus | Governmental funds: current financial resources | Economic resources |
| Basis of accounting | Governmental funds: modified accrual | Accrual |
| Capital assets in governmental funds | Not reported (expenditure when purchased) | N/A |
| Revenue recognition | Measurable and available | ASC 606 five-step model |
| Net position categories | Net investment in capital assets, restricted, unrestricted | Stockholders' equity (common stock, APIC, RE) |
| Budget reporting | Required (RSBI) | Not required |
| Pensions | GASB 68 (net pension liability) | ASC 715 (projected benefit obligation) |

## Budgetary Accounting

Governmental funds record the **legally adopted budget** in the accounts:

- Debit: Estimated revenues
- Credit: Appropriations (authorized expenditures)
- Debit/Credit: Budgetary fund balance (difference)

At year-end, the budgetary entry is **reversed**. The budget-to-actual comparison is presented as **Required Supplementary Information (RSI)** for the general fund and major special revenue funds with legally adopted budgets.

Encumbrance accounting reserves fund balance for **purchase orders and contracts** not yet fulfilled. Encumbrances are not GAAP expenditures — they are budgetary controls.

## Key Terms

- **Modified accrual** — The accounting basis used for governmental funds where revenues are recognized when measurable and available, and expenditures when the fund liability is incurred
- **Net pension liability** — The difference between the total pension liability and the fair value of plan assets, reported on the government-wide statement of net position
- **Infrastructure assets** — Long-lived capital assets that are stationary and normally maintained for a significantly longer period than other capital assets (roads, bridges, water systems)
- **Encumbrance** — A budgetary reservation of fund balance for outstanding purchase commitments; not a GAAP expenditure

## Practice Problems

**1.** Under the modified accrual basis, when are property tax revenues recognized?

a) When levied, regardless of collection
b) When the cash is received
c) When measurable and available (typically collected within 60 days of year-end)
d) Ratably over the fiscal year

**Answer:** c) When measurable and available — Property taxes are recognized in the period for which they are levied, but only to the extent they are collected within the availability period (typically 60 days after year-end). Amounts collected later are deferred.

---

**2.** Which of the following fund types uses the full accrual basis of accounting?

a) General fund
b) Capital projects fund
c) Enterprise fund
d) Debt service fund

**Answer:** c) Enterprise fund — Enterprise funds are proprietary funds that use full accrual accounting and the economic resources measurement focus. All governmental funds (general, capital projects, debt service, special revenue, permanent) use modified accrual.

---

**3.** A government issues $10,000,000 in general obligation bonds at 102. In the governmental fund financial statements, what amount is reported as an other financing source?

a) $10,000,000
b) $10,200,000
c) $200,000
d) $10,000,000 with the $200,000 premium reported separately as an other financing source

**Answer:** d) $10,000,000 with the $200,000 premium reported separately — In governmental funds, bond proceeds are reported as an other financing source of $10,000,000, and the premium of $200,000 is reported as a separate other financing source. In the government-wide statements, the premium would be amortized.
