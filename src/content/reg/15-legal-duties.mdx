# Legal Duties and Responsibilities

## Learning Objectives

- Understand the scope and limitations of tax practitioner privileged communications under IRC Section 7525
- Distinguish between the work product doctrine and the attorney-client privilege as they apply to tax practice
- Calculate preparer penalties under Section 6694 for unreasonable positions and willful/reckless conduct
- Apply due diligence requirements under Section 6695 and identify preparer identification obligations
- Recognize how Circular 230 duties connect to statutory penalty provisions
- Identify situations where privilege does not apply, including criminal matters and tax shelters

## Tax Practitioner Privileged Communications (Section 7525)

The **IRC Section 7525 privilege** extends the protections of attorney-client privilege to **tax advice** provided by any **federally authorized tax practitioner** — including CPAs, enrolled agents, and enrolled actuaries — in noncriminal tax matters before the IRS and in federal court proceedings.

### Scope of the Privilege

The Section 7525 privilege protects confidential communications between a taxpayer and a federally authorized tax practitioner that involve **tax advice**. The communication must be made in the context of the practitioner providing advice on tax matters, not merely tax return preparation.

| Element | Requirement |
|---------|-------------|
| Who is covered | CPAs, enrolled agents, enrolled actuaries (not all preparers) |
| What is covered | Tax advice communications (not return preparation) |
| Where it applies | Noncriminal proceedings before the IRS and in federal courts |
| When it does not apply | Criminal tax matters, state proceedings, tax shelter transactions |

### Key Limitations

1. **Criminal matters** — Section 7525 does not apply in **criminal tax proceedings**. If the IRS pursues a criminal investigation, the practitioner-client privilege does not protect communications. Only the attorney-client privilege (for attorneys) applies in criminal matters.

2. **Tax shelter transactions** — The privilege does not apply to any written communication between a tax practitioner and:
   - A director, shareholder, officer, or employee of a corporation in connection with promoting participation in a **tax shelter** (as defined under Section 6662(d)(2)(C))
   - Any person in connection with organizing, promoting, or participating in a tax shelter

3. **State proceedings** — Section 7525 is a federal privilege and does not extend to state tax proceedings. State-level protections vary by jurisdiction.

4. **Third-party communications** — The privilege is waived if the taxpayer discloses the communication to a third party (other than certain authorized persons). Sharing privileged tax advice with a bank, investor, or business partner typically destroys the privilege.

> Exam tip: Section 7525 only protects tax advice, not tax return preparation. A CPA who prepares a return and provides no advisory communications cannot invoke Section 7525 to shield workpapers related purely to the preparation of the return. Also remember that the privilege never applies in criminal matters for non-attorneys.

## Work Product Doctrine

The **work product doctrine** protects materials prepared by a practitioner **in anticipation of litigation**. Unlike the Section 7525 privilege, the work product doctrine is not limited to communications — it extends to the practitioner's notes, memoranda, mental impressions, conclusions, and legal theories.

### Two Levels of Protection

| Level | What Is Protected | Standard to Overcome |
|-------|------------------|---------------------|
| **Ordinary work product** | Factual compilations, research, and analysis prepared in anticipation of litigation | Opposing party must show **substantial need** and inability to obtain the equivalent without **undue hardship** |
| **Opinion work product** | Mental impressions, conclusions, opinions, and legal theories of the practitioner | **Nearly absolute** protection — almost never discoverable |

### Application to Tax Practice

The work product doctrine is narrower in practice than Section 7525 because it requires that the materials be prepared **in anticipation of litigation**, not merely for tax advice or compliance purposes. A memo analyzing a client's tax position prepared during routine planning would not qualify, but the same memo prepared after receiving an IRS audit notice likely would.

**Worked Example:**

A CPA prepares a memo analyzing a client's aggressive deduction position. Consider two scenarios:

| Scenario | Protected by Work Product? | Reasoning |
|----------|---------------------------|-----------|
| Memo prepared during annual tax planning | No | Not prepared in anticipation of litigation |
| Memo prepared after receiving IRS 30-day letter | Yes | Prepared in anticipation of potential Tax Court litigation |

> Exam tip: The work product doctrine requires the "anticipation of litigation" trigger. Tax return workpapers prepared in the normal course of business are not protected work product. However, analysis prepared after an audit begins or after receiving an adverse ruling may qualify.

## Preparer Penalties — Section 6694

Section 6694 imposes penalties on **tax return preparers** who take improper positions on returns. The penalties operate on a two-tier system based on the preparer's level of culpability.

### Section 6694(a): Unreasonable Positions

A penalty applies if a preparer takes a position on a return that does not meet the required standard and the position results in an **understatement of tax liability**.

**Standard required:**

| Situation | Position Standard |
|-----------|------------------|
| Positions not disclosed | Must have **substantial authority** (approximately 40% likelihood of being sustained on the merits) |
| Disclosed positions | Must have a **reasonable basis** (approximately 20% likelihood) |
| Tax shelter or reportable transactions | Must be **more likely than not** correct (>50% likelihood), regardless of disclosure |

**Penalty amount:** The **greater of $1,000 or 50% of the income** derived by the preparer from preparing the return.

**Defense:** The penalty does not apply if the preparer demonstrates **reasonable cause** and acted in **good faith**.

### Section 6694(b): Willful or Reckless Conduct

A higher penalty applies if the understatement is due to:

- **Willful attempt** to understate tax liability
- **Reckless or intentional disregard** of rules or regulations

**Penalty amount:** The **greater of $5,000 or 75% of the income** derived by the preparer from preparing the return.

This penalty applies regardless of whether the position was disclosed. There is **no reasonable cause defense** for willful or reckless conduct. The Section 6694(b) penalty is reduced by any amount paid under Section 6694(a) for the same position.

**Worked Example:**

A CPA prepares a return and earns a fee of $3,000. The CPA takes a position that has only a 10% chance of being sustained without disclosing it.

| Penalty Analysis | Calculation |
|-----------------|-------------|
| Required standard (undisclosed) | Substantial authority (~40%) |
| Actual likelihood | 10% — fails substantial authority |
| 6694(a) penalty — $1,000 or 50% of income | Greater of $1,000 or $1,500 = **$1,500** |

If the same CPA willfully understated the liability:

| Penalty Analysis | Calculation |
|-----------------|-------------|
| 6694(b) penalty — $5,000 or 75% of income | Greater of $5,000 or $2,250 = **$5,000** |
| Less: 6694(a) amount already paid | ($1,500) |
| **Net 6694(b) penalty** | **$3,500** |

> Exam tip: Know the three position standards: reasonable basis (~20%) for disclosed positions, substantial authority (~40%) for undisclosed positions, and more likely than not (>50%) for tax shelters. The exam frequently tests which standard applies in a given scenario.

## Due Diligence Requirements — Section 6695

Section 6695 imposes penalties on preparers for failing to meet certain administrative and due diligence requirements. These are separate from the substantive position penalties under Section 6694.

### Administrative Penalties

| Violation | Penalty (2025) |
|-----------|---------------|
| Failure to furnish copy of return to taxpayer | $55 per failure |
| Failure to sign the return | $55 per failure |
| Failure to furnish PTIN (Preparer Tax Identification Number) | $55 per failure |
| Failure to retain copy or list of returns prepared | $55 per failure |
| Failure to file correct information returns (Form 8867) | $55 per failure |
| Maximum aggregate penalty per calendar year (per category) | $28,000 |

### Earned Income Credit Due Diligence (Section 6695(g))

Preparers claiming certain credits on behalf of a client must satisfy enhanced **due diligence requirements**. The credits subject to the due diligence penalty include:

- Earned Income Credit (EIC)
- Child Tax Credit (CTC) / Additional Child Tax Credit / Other Dependent Credit
- American Opportunity Tax Credit (AOTC)
- Head of Household filing status

**Due diligence penalty:** **$600 per failure** per return for each applicable credit or filing status for which due diligence is not satisfied.

A preparer who fails to meet due diligence requirements for both the EIC and the CTC on the same return faces a penalty of **$1,200** ($600 + $600).

**Due diligence requirements include:**

1. Complete and submit **Form 8867** (Paid Preparer's Due Diligence Checklist)
2. Complete the applicable **worksheets** or record the alternative computation
3. Make **reasonable inquiries** to determine the taxpayer's eligibility and correct amounts
4. **Retain records** of the information used to determine eligibility for 3 years
5. Do not know (or have reason to know) that the information is **incorrect or incomplete**

> Exam tip: The due diligence penalty ($600) is per credit per return. A return claiming three applicable credits without proper due diligence could trigger $1,800 in penalties. This is frequently tested alongside the administrative penalties.

## Preparer Identification Requirements

All **paid tax return preparers** must obtain and use a **Preparer Tax Identification Number (PTIN)** on every return they prepare for compensation. The requirement applies regardless of whether the preparer is a CPA, enrolled agent, or unenrolled preparer.

**Key rules:**

- A PTIN must be renewed annually
- Failure to include a PTIN on a prepared return is subject to a **$55 penalty per failure** under Section 6695
- A preparer's Social Security Number must **never** be used in place of a PTIN on client returns
- The **signing preparer** is the individual with primary responsibility for the return's overall substantive accuracy

### Who Is a Tax Return Preparer?

A **tax return preparer** is any person who prepares for compensation, or employs others to prepare for compensation, all or a **substantial portion** of a tax return or claim for refund. Merely typing, reproducing, or providing mechanical assistance does not make a person a preparer.

| Is a Tax Return Preparer | Is NOT a Tax Return Preparer |
|--------------------------|------------------------------|
| CPA who determines the tax treatment of a transaction | Data entry clerk who inputs numbers provided by the taxpayer |
| Enrolled agent who signs and files returns | Volunteer under IRS VITA program |
| Attorney providing substantive tax advice reflected on the return | Employee preparing their employer's return as part of regular duties |

## Circular 230 Connection to Statutory Duties

Circular 230 (Treasury Department regulations governing practice before the IRS) and the statutory penalty provisions under Sections 6694-6695 are **complementary but distinct** systems.

### Key Overlaps and Differences

| Aspect | Circular 230 | Section 6694/6695 |
|--------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Enforced by** | Office of Professional Responsibility (OPR) | IRS examination and penalty divisions |
| **Penalties** | Censure, suspension, disbarment, monetary penalty | Dollar penalties per return or per failure |
| **Position standard** | Reasonable basis for undisclosed positions; more likely than not for tax shelters | Substantial authority (undisclosed) or reasonable basis (disclosed) |
| **Scope** | Governs all conduct before the IRS, including advice, representation, and filings | Applies specifically to return preparation and specific administrative duties |
| **Due diligence** | General due diligence obligation in all matters | Specific credit-by-credit due diligence requirements |

A preparer can face **both** Circular 230 sanctions and statutory penalties for the same conduct. For example, a CPA who signs a return without reviewing the underlying information could face a Section 6694(a) penalty for an unreasonable position AND a Circular 230 disciplinary proceeding for lack of due diligence.

**Worked Example:**

A CPA prepares a return claiming $15,000 in charitable deductions without obtaining or reviewing any substantiation. The deduction is later disallowed.

| Consequence | Basis |
|-------------|-------|
| Section 6694(a) penalty | Position lacked substantial authority (no documentation) |
| Section 6695(g) penalty (if EIC also claimed) | Failed to meet due diligence requirements |
| Circular 230 discipline | Failure to exercise due diligence in preparing the return |

> Exam tip: The exam tests whether you can distinguish Circular 230 from statutory penalties. Remember that Circular 230 governs practice before the IRS broadly (advice, representation, filings), while Section 6694 focuses specifically on return positions. Both can apply to the same conduct, but the enforcement mechanisms and penalties are different.

## Key Terms

- **Section 7525 privilege** — federally authorized tax practitioner privilege extending attorney-client style protection to tax advice in noncriminal proceedings before the IRS and in federal court
- **Work product doctrine** — protection for materials (notes, memos, analysis) prepared by a practitioner in anticipation of litigation
- **Substantial authority** — the standard for undisclosed return positions under Section 6694(a), requiring approximately a 40% probability of being sustained on the merits
- **Reasonable basis** — the minimum standard for disclosed return positions, requiring approximately a 20% probability of being sustained
- **More likely than not** — the standard for tax shelter positions, requiring greater than 50% probability of being sustained
- **Section 6694(a) penalty** — the greater of $1,000 or 50% of preparer income for taking an unreasonable position resulting in an understatement
- **Section 6694(b) penalty** — the greater of $5,000 or 75% of preparer income for willful or reckless conduct resulting in an understatement
- **Section 6695 penalties** — administrative penalties ($55 per failure) for failure to sign returns, furnish PTINs, provide copies, or retain records
- **Due diligence penalty (Section 6695(g))** — $600 per credit per return for failure to meet due diligence requirements for EIC, CTC, AOTC, and head of household
- **PTIN** — Preparer Tax Identification Number, required for all paid tax return preparers and renewed annually
- **Signing preparer** — the individual preparer who has primary responsibility for the overall substantive accuracy of a tax return

## Practice Problems

**1.** A CPA provides tax advice to a client regarding a complex deduction. The IRS later subpoenas the CPA's communications with the client. Under what circumstances does the Section 7525 privilege NOT apply?

a) The matter involves a noncriminal tax proceeding before the IRS
b) The matter involves a criminal tax investigation
c) The CPA is an enrolled agent rather than a licensed CPA
d) The communication was made during a face-to-face meeting

**Answer:** b) The matter involves a criminal tax investigation — Section 7525 privilege does not apply in criminal tax proceedings. The privilege is limited to noncriminal tax matters before the IRS and in federal court.

---

**2.** A tax preparer takes an undisclosed position on a return that has only a 25% chance of being sustained on the merits. What penalty applies under Section 6694?

a) No penalty because the position has a reasonable basis
b) Section 6694(a) penalty: the greater of $1,000 or 50% of preparer income
c) Section 6694(b) penalty: the greater of $5,000 or 75% of preparer income
d) Section 6695 penalty: $55 per failure

**Answer:** b) Section 6694(a) penalty — An undisclosed position must meet the substantial authority standard (approximately 40%). A 25% likelihood falls below substantial authority but above reasonable basis. Since the position was not disclosed, the 6694(a) penalty applies.

---

**3.** A preparer earns $4,000 for preparing a return and willfully understates the client's tax liability. What is the Section 6694(b) penalty?

a) $1,000
b) $3,000
c) $4,000
d) $5,000

**Answer:** d) $5,000 — The Section 6694(b) penalty is the greater of $5,000 or 75% of preparer income. 75% of $4,000 = $3,000, which is less than $5,000, so the penalty is $5,000.

---

**4.** A preparer claims both the EIC and the Child Tax Credit on a client's return without completing Form 8867 or making reasonable inquiries. What is the total due diligence penalty?

a) $55
b) $600
c) $1,200
d) $1,800

**Answer:** c) $1,200 — The due diligence penalty under Section 6695(g) is $600 per applicable credit per return. Failing to satisfy due diligence for both the EIC and the CTC results in a $1,200 penalty ($600 + $600).

---

**5.** A CPA prepares a memo analyzing a client's uncertain tax position. The memo was prepared as part of routine year-end tax planning before any audit or dispute arose. Is the memo protected by the work product doctrine?

a) Yes, because it was prepared by a CPA
b) Yes, because it contains legal analysis
c) No, because it was not prepared in anticipation of litigation
d) No, because the Section 7525 privilege supersedes the work product doctrine

**Answer:** c) No, because it was not prepared in anticipation of litigation — The work product doctrine only protects materials prepared in anticipation of litigation. Routine tax planning memos do not qualify. The memo might be protected under Section 7525 as tax advice, but that is a separate analysis.
