# C Corporations

## Learning Objectives

- Calculate corporate taxable income and the flat 21% tax liability
- Understand the tax consequences of corporate formation under Section 351
- Distinguish between dividends, return of capital, and capital gain distributions
- Identify the accumulated earnings tax and personal holding company tax triggers

## Corporate Formation Under Section 351

When a corporation is formed, transferors may contribute property in exchange for stock. Under **Section 351**, no gain or loss is recognized if:

1. **Property** is transferred to the corporation (services do not count as property)
2. In exchange for **stock** of the corporation
3. The transferor(s) are in **control** of the corporation immediately after the exchange (control means owning at least **80%** of total voting power and 80% of all other classes of stock)

If the transferor receives **boot** (cash or other non-stock property) in addition to stock, gain is recognized to the extent of the boot received but never in excess of the realized gain. No loss is recognized in a Section 351 exchange.

**Worked Example:**

Adams transfers equipment (basis $40,000, FMV $100,000) to a new corporation in exchange for 100% of the stock and $15,000 cash.

| Step | Calculation | Amount |
|------|------------|--------|
| FMV of stock received | $100,000 - $15,000 | $85,000 |
| Cash (boot) received | | $15,000 |
| Amount realized | $85,000 + $15,000 | $100,000 |
| Adjusted basis of property transferred | | ($40,000) |
| Realized gain | | $60,000 |
| **Recognized gain** | Lesser of boot or realized gain | **$15,000** |

**Shareholder's basis in stock:** $40,000 (basis of property) - $15,000 (boot received) + $15,000 (gain recognized) = **$40,000**

**Corporation's basis in equipment:** $40,000 (transferor's basis) + $15,000 (gain recognized by transferor) = **$55,000**

> Exam tip: The Section 351 control requirement is tested frequently. Remember that control means 80% of voting power AND 80% of each class of nonvoting stock. A person contributing only services does not count toward the control group.

## Calculating Corporate Taxable Income

A C corporation computes taxable income similarly to individuals but with important differences. The corporation files **Form 1120** and pays tax at a flat **21% rate**.

### Gross Income

Corporate gross income includes all income from whatever source derived: sales revenue, service revenue, interest, dividends, rents, royalties, and gains from property dispositions.

### Deductions

Corporations can deduct ordinary and necessary business expenses, including:

- **Compensation** — salaries, wages, bonuses (must be reasonable; excessive compensation may be reclassified as a constructive dividend)
- **Rent and lease payments**
- **Depreciation and amortization**
- **Bad debts** — accrual-method corporations may use the specific charge-off method
- **Charitable contributions** — limited to **10% of taxable income** computed before the charitable deduction, the dividends received deduction, NOL carrybacks, and capital loss carrybacks. Excess contributions carry forward five years.
- **Net operating losses (NOLs)** — deductible up to **80% of taxable income** in the carryforward year, with indefinite carryforward

### Dividends Received Deduction (DRD)

When one corporation owns stock in another domestic corporation and receives dividends, a **dividends received deduction** reduces or eliminates double taxation of corporate earnings:

| Ownership Percentage | DRD Percentage |
|---------------------|---------------|
| Less than 20% | 50% |
| 20% to less than 80% | 65% |
| 80% or more (affiliated group) | 100% |

The DRD is generally limited to the applicable percentage of the corporation's taxable income (computed without the DRD, NOL deduction, and capital loss carryback), unless the DRD creates or increases an NOL.

**Worked Example:**

Beta Corp owns 30% of Gamma Corp stock and receives $100,000 in dividends. Beta Corp's taxable income before the DRD is $150,000.

| Step | Calculation | Amount |
|------|------------|--------|
| Dividends received | | $100,000 |
| DRD percentage (20-80% ownership) | | 65% |
| DRD (before limitation) | $100,000 x 65% | $65,000 |
| Taxable income limitation | $150,000 x 65% | $97,500 |
| **Actual DRD** | Lesser of $65,000 or $97,500 | **$65,000** |

### Corporate Capital Gains and Losses

- Corporate capital gains are taxed at the same flat **21% rate** as ordinary income (no preferential rate)
- Capital losses can only offset **capital gains** — they cannot offset ordinary income
- Unused capital losses carry back **three years** and forward **five years**, treated as short-term in the carryover year

## Distributions to Shareholders

When a C corporation distributes cash or property to shareholders, the tax treatment depends on the corporation's **earnings and profits (E&P)**:

### Distribution Ordering Rules

1. **Dividend** — taxable to the extent of current and accumulated E&P (taxed at qualified dividend rates of 0%, 15%, or 20% for individuals)
2. **Return of capital** — reduces the shareholder's basis in the stock (not taxable)
3. **Capital gain** — any distribution exceeding E&P and stock basis is treated as capital gain

**Worked Example:**

Delta Corp has accumulated E&P of $30,000 and current E&P of $10,000. It distributes $60,000 to its sole shareholder, whose stock basis is $15,000.

| Layer | Amount | Tax Treatment |
|-------|--------|--------------|
| Current E&P | $10,000 | Dividend |
| Accumulated E&P | $30,000 | Dividend |
| Return of capital (reduces basis from $15,000 to $0) | $15,000 | Not taxable |
| Excess | $5,000 | Capital gain |
| **Total distribution** | **$60,000** | |

> Exam tip: Current E&P is allocated pro rata to all distributions made during the year. Accumulated E&P is applied chronologically. This distinction matters when there are multiple distributions in a year.

## Accumulated Earnings Tax and Personal Holding Company Tax

### Accumulated Earnings Tax (Section 531)

The **accumulated earnings tax** is a penalty tax of **20%** imposed on corporations that accumulate earnings beyond the reasonable needs of the business to help shareholders avoid dividend taxation.

- Applies to C corporations (not S corps, tax-exempt organizations, or personal holding companies)
- **Accumulated earnings credit**: corporations may accumulate up to **$250,000** ($150,000 for certain service corporations) without justification
- Reasonable business needs include planned expansion, debt retirement, working capital, and self-insurance reserves
- The tax is on the **accumulated taxable income** (taxable income minus taxes, dividends paid, and the accumulated earnings credit)

### Personal Holding Company Tax (Section 541)

The **personal holding company (PHC) tax** is a **20% penalty tax** on undistributed personal holding company income. A corporation is a PHC if:

1. **Stock ownership test** — more than 50% of the stock is owned by five or fewer individuals at any time during the last half of the tax year
2. **Income test** — at least 60% of adjusted ordinary gross income consists of PHC income (dividends, interest, rents below 50% of AOGI, royalties, personal service contracts)

The PHC tax can be avoided by distributing the PHC income as dividends, including **consent dividends** or **deficiency dividends**.

## Key Terms

- **Section 351** — the Code provision allowing tax-free incorporation when property is transferred for stock and the transferors have 80% control
- **Dividends received deduction (DRD)** — a deduction that reduces the double taxation of corporate dividends received by a corporate shareholder
- **Earnings and profits (E&P)** — a tax concept measuring a corporation's economic ability to pay dividends; determines dividend treatment of distributions
- **Accumulated earnings tax** — a 20% penalty tax on corporations that retain earnings beyond reasonable business needs to avoid shareholder-level tax
- **Constructive dividend** — a payment or benefit to a shareholder that is reclassified as a dividend (e.g., excessive compensation, personal expenses paid by the corporation)

## Practice Problems

**1.** A taxpayer transfers property with an adjusted basis of $50,000 and FMV of $80,000 to a new corporation in exchange for 100% of the stock. No boot is received. Under Section 351, what is the corporation's basis in the property?

a) $0
b) $50,000
c) $65,000
d) $80,000

**Answer:** b) $50,000 — In a Section 351 exchange, the corporation takes a carryover basis equal to the transferor's adjusted basis ($50,000) plus any gain recognized by the transferor ($0, since no boot was received). The corporation's basis is $50,000.

---

**2.** Zeta Corp, which owns 15% of Omega Corp, receives $200,000 in dividends from Omega. What is Zeta's dividends received deduction?

a) $0
b) $100,000
c) $130,000
d) $200,000

**Answer:** b) $100,000 — With less than 20% ownership, the DRD percentage is 50%. The deduction is $200,000 x 50% = $100,000 (assuming no taxable income limitation applies).

---

**3.** A C corporation distributes $50,000 to its sole shareholder. The corporation has $20,000 of current and accumulated E&P. The shareholder's stock basis is $25,000. How is the distribution treated?

a) $50,000 dividend
b) $20,000 dividend, $25,000 return of capital, $5,000 capital gain
c) $20,000 dividend, $30,000 return of capital
d) $25,000 dividend, $25,000 capital gain

**Answer:** b) $20,000 dividend, $25,000 return of capital, $5,000 capital gain — The distribution is first a dividend to the extent of E&P ($20,000), then a tax-free return of capital reducing stock basis to zero ($25,000), and finally capital gain for the excess ($5,000).
