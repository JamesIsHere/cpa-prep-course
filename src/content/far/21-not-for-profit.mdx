# Not-for-Profit Accounting

## Learning Objectives

- Understand the financial reporting framework for not-for-profit entities under ASC 958
- Distinguish between contributions with and without donor restrictions
- Apply revenue recognition principles for contributions, grants, and exchange transactions
- Prepare and interpret the required financial statements for NFP entities

## Overview of NFP Financial Reporting

**Not-for-profit (NFP)** entities follow FASB standards (not GASB), but with specialized guidance under **ASC 958**. NFPs include charities, foundations, private universities, voluntary health and welfare organizations, religious organizations, and similar entities that receive significant resources from donors who do not expect a financial return.

The key conceptual difference from for-profit accounting is the absence of owners' equity. Instead, NFPs report **net assets**, classified into two categories:

- **Net assets without donor restrictions** — Resources available for any purpose at the board's discretion
- **Net assets with donor restrictions** — Resources subject to donor-imposed restrictions, either purpose restrictions (must be used for a specified purpose) or time restrictions (must be used in or after a specified period)

> ASU 2016-14 simplified net asset classification from three categories to two. The old categories — unrestricted, temporarily restricted, and permanently restricted — were replaced by "without donor restrictions" and "with donor restrictions." The exam uses the current two-category model.

## Required Financial Statements

NFP entities prepare four financial statements:

1. **Statement of Financial Position** — Similar to a balance sheet. Reports total assets, total liabilities, and net assets (classified as with or without donor restrictions).

2. **Statement of Activities** — Reports revenues, expenses, gains, and losses, showing changes in each net asset category. Must present expenses by both **functional classification** (program services vs. supporting activities) and **natural classification** (salaries, rent, supplies). The functional breakdown can be shown on the face of the statement or in the notes.

3. **Statement of Cash Flows** — Follows the same format as for-profit entities (operating, investing, financing activities). NFPs may use either the direct or indirect method.

4. **Statement of Functional Expenses** — Reports expenses by both function and nature in a matrix format. Required for voluntary health and welfare organizations and encouraged for all NFPs under ASU 2016-14.

## Contributions

A **contribution** is an unconditional transfer of assets to an NFP, with no expectation of receiving anything of equal value in return. Contributions are the primary revenue source for most NFPs.

### Unconditional Contributions

Recognized as revenue in the period received or pledged. The classification depends on donor restrictions:

- **No restriction** — Revenue without donor restrictions
- **Purpose or time restriction** — Revenue with donor restrictions

When a restriction is satisfied (the purpose is fulfilled or the time period elapses), the NFP reclassifies the amount from net assets with donor restrictions to net assets without donor restrictions. This reclassification is reported as **net assets released from restrictions** on the statement of activities.

### Conditional Contributions

A **conditional contribution** depends on a future and uncertain event — a barrier that must be overcome and a right of return exists if the condition is not met. Conditional contributions are **not recognized as revenue** until the conditions are substantially met. Until then, amounts received are recorded as a **refundable advance** (liability).

**Example:** A foundation pledges $500,000 to a university on the condition that the university raises $500,000 in matching funds. Until the university meets the matching requirement, the pledge is conditional and not recognized as revenue.

> The distinction between a condition and a restriction is critical on the exam. A condition involves a barrier plus a right of return or release — it delays recognition. A restriction limits how already-recognized revenue can be used — it affects classification, not timing.

### Contributed Services

Contributed services are recognized as revenue only if they:

1. Create or enhance a **nonfinancial asset**, or
2. Require **specialized skills**, are provided by individuals possessing those skills, and would typically need to be purchased if not donated

**Example:** A CPA volunteers to perform the annual audit for a charity. This is recognized as contribution revenue and a corresponding expense because it requires specialized skills and would otherwise be purchased. A volunteer who answers phones at a fundraising event would not be recognized.

### Contributed Nonfinancial Assets (In-Kind Donations)

Under ASU 2020-07, NFPs must present contributed nonfinancial assets as a **separate line item** on the statement of activities and provide detailed disclosures about the valuation techniques used, donor restrictions, and whether the assets were monetized or used in operations.

## Exchange Transactions vs. Contributions

Not all revenue received by an NFP is a contribution. **Exchange transactions** — where both parties give and receive approximately equal value — are accounted for under the same standards as for-profit entities (e.g., ASC 606 for revenue from contracts with customers).

| Transaction | Classification |
|------------|---------------|
| Donation with no strings attached | Contribution — without donor restrictions |
| Grant requiring specific research outcomes | Conditional contribution (barrier + right of return) |
| Tuition charged by a private university | Exchange transaction (ASC 606) |
| Membership dues with no tangible benefits | Contribution |
| Membership dues with significant benefits | Exchange transaction |
| Government grant for general operations | Contribution — may have restrictions |

## Endowments

An **endowment** is a contribution where the donor stipulates that the principal must be maintained permanently (or for a specified term). Only the investment return may be spent.

- The principal is reported as net assets **with donor restrictions** (permanently restricted under old terminology)
- Investment income follows the donor's stipulation and applicable state law (UPMIFA). Unrestricted income increases net assets without donor restrictions; restricted income increases net assets with donor restrictions.

## Key Terms

- **Net assets without donor restrictions** — Resources available for any purpose at the discretion of the NFP's governing board
- **Net assets with donor restrictions** — Resources subject to donor-imposed purpose or time restrictions
- **Conditional contribution** — A contribution that depends on a future and uncertain event; not recognized until conditions are substantially met
- **Net assets released from restrictions** — A reclassification on the statement of activities when a donor restriction is satisfied
- **Functional classification** — Grouping expenses by their purpose: program services (mission-related) versus supporting activities (management, fundraising)
