# Government-Wide and Fund Financial Statements

## Learning Objectives

- Distinguish between government-wide and fund financial statements
- Understand the GASB 34 reporting model and its dual-perspective approach
- Prepare reconciliations between fund statements and government-wide statements
- Apply GASB 68 (pensions) and GASB 75 (OPEB) reporting requirements

## Dual-Perspective Reporting

GASB requires **dual-perspective reporting** — two sets of financial statements that provide different views of the government's finances. This approach satisfies both fiscal accountability (through fund statements) and operational accountability (through government-wide statements).

## Government-Wide Financial Statements

Government-wide statements present the government as a whole using the **full accrual basis** and **economic resources** measurement focus. They include:

- **Statement of Net Position** — Similar to a balance sheet. Reports assets + deferred outflows − liabilities − deferred inflows = net position. Net position is classified as: net investment in capital assets, restricted, or unrestricted.
- **Statement of Activities** — Reports expenses by function/program, offset by program revenues (charges for services, operating grants, capital grants). The difference is the net expense, which is funded by general revenues (taxes, unrestricted grants).

Government-wide statements separate activities into **governmental activities** and **business-type activities**. Fiduciary funds are excluded because the resources do not belong to the government.

### Net Position Categories

| Category | Definition |
|----------|-----------|
| Net investment in capital assets | Capital assets minus related debt minus unspent bond proceeds |
| Restricted | Resources subject to external constraints (legal, contractual, or from grantors/creditors) |
| Unrestricted | All remaining net position — may be designated by the government but is not externally restricted |

## Fund Financial Statements

Fund financial statements present individual funds using the measurement focus and basis of accounting appropriate to each fund type:

| Fund Type | Basis of Accounting | Measurement Focus |
|-----------|-------------------|------------------|
| Governmental funds | Modified accrual | Current financial resources |
| Proprietary funds | Full accrual | Economic resources |
| Fiduciary funds | Full accrual | Economic resources |

### Major Fund Reporting

GASB requires separate column presentation for each **major fund** in the governmental and proprietary fund statements. A fund is major if it meets both:

1. Total assets/deferred outflows, liabilities/deferred inflows, revenues, or expenditures/expenses of that fund are at least **10%** of the corresponding total for all funds of that category (governmental or enterprise)
2. The same element is at least **5%** of the corresponding total for all governmental and enterprise funds combined

The General Fund is **always** reported as a major fund. All nonmajor funds are aggregated in a single "Other Governmental Funds" column.

## Reconciliation Between Fund and Government-Wide Statements

The key difference between modified accrual (fund) and full accrual (government-wide) statements creates reconciling items that must be presented. The most common reconciling adjustments:

| Item | Fund Statement | Government-Wide Adjustment |
|------|---------------|---------------------------|
| Capital asset purchases | Reported as expenditures | Capitalize the asset; remove expenditure |
| Depreciation | Not recorded | Record depreciation expense |
| Bond proceeds | Other financing source (revenue-like) | Record as long-term liability |
| Bond principal payments | Reported as expenditures | Reduce long-term liability; remove expenditure |
| Accrued interest on long-term debt | Not recorded until due | Record accrued interest payable |
| Internal service fund activities | Separate fund | Typically consolidated with governmental activities |

### Worked Example — Reconciliation

A government's General Fund reports total fund balance of $2,400,000. The following adjustments are needed:

| Reconciling Item | Amount |
|-----------------|--------|
| Fund balance — General Fund | $2,400,000 |
| Add: Capital assets (net of depreciation) | 8,500,000 |
| Less: Long-term debt outstanding | (5,200,000) |
| Less: Accrued interest payable | (130,000) |
| Add: Internal service fund net position | 350,000 |
| Add: Deferred property tax revenue (available > 60 days) | 180,000 |
| **Net position — governmental activities** | **$6,100,000** |

> Reconciliation questions are heavily tested. The key insight is that governmental funds exclude capital assets and long-term liabilities — the reconciliation adds them back to arrive at the full accrual government-wide picture.

## GASB 34 — The Reporting Model

**GASB Statement No. 34** established the current financial reporting model for state and local governments, requiring the dual-perspective approach. Key requirements include:

- **Management's Discussion and Analysis (MD&A)** — Required supplementary information providing an analytical overview of financial activities. Presented before the basic financial statements.
- **Government-wide financial statements** — Statement of Net Position and Statement of Activities using full accrual, economic resources focus
- **Fund financial statements** — Separate statements for governmental, proprietary, and fiduciary funds
- **Reconciliations** — Required between government-wide and fund statements
- **Infrastructure assets** — Governments must report major infrastructure assets (roads, bridges, water systems). They may use either the **depreciation approach** or the **modified approach** (report maintenance costs rather than depreciation if the government maintains the asset at or above an established condition level).
- **Budgetary comparison** — Required supplementary information comparing original budget, final budget, and actual results for the general fund and major special revenue funds

## Pension Accounting — GASB 68

**GASB 68** governs pension reporting for state and local government employers that participate in defined benefit pension plans.

### Net Pension Liability (or Asset)

On the government-wide Statement of Net Position, employers report a **net pension liability** (or asset):

**Net Pension Liability = Total Pension Liability − Plan Fiduciary Net Position**

- **Total pension liability (TPL)** — The actuarial present value of projected pension benefits attributed to past service
- **Plan fiduciary net position** — The fair value of assets held in the pension trust

### Pension Expense

Annual pension expense includes:
- **Service cost** — Present value of benefits earned by employees in the current period
- **Interest on total pension liability** — TPL beginning balance × discount rate
- **Changes in benefit terms** — Recognized immediately
- **Projected earnings on plan assets** — Expected investment return (reduces expense)
- **Amortization of deferred outflows/inflows** — Differences between expected and actual experience, and changes in assumptions, are recognized in pension expense over a closed period (typically the average remaining service life of employees)

### Deferred Outflows and Deferred Inflows

Differences between expected and actual results are **not** recognized immediately in pension expense. Instead, they are reported as **deferred outflows of resources** or **deferred inflows of resources** and amortized into expense over future periods:

- Difference between projected and actual earnings on plan assets → amortized over 5 years
- Changes in assumptions and experience differences → amortized over average remaining service life
- Employer contributions made **after** the measurement date → reported as deferred outflow and recognized next period

> GASB 68 pension questions frequently test the net pension liability calculation and the treatment of deferred outflows/inflows. Remember: plan assets are subtracted from the total pension liability — they are not reported separately on the employer's balance sheet.

## OPEB — GASB 75

**GASB 75** mirrors GASB 68 but applies to **other postemployment benefits (OPEB)** — primarily retiree health insurance. The framework is identical:

- Report a **net OPEB liability (or asset)** = Total OPEB liability − Plan fiduciary net position
- OPEB expense includes service cost, interest, and amortization of deferred amounts
- Deferred outflows and inflows are used for experience differences and assumption changes
- The discount rate depends on whether the plan is funded (use expected return on assets) or unfunded (use a high-quality municipal bond index rate)

The main difference from pensions is that many governments have **unfunded** OPEB plans (no trust assets), which means the net OPEB liability equals the full total OPEB liability — often a very large number.

## Key Terms

- **Net position** — The government-wide equivalent of equity, classified as net investment in capital assets, restricted, or unrestricted
- **Government-wide statements** — Financial statements presenting the government as a whole using full accrual and economic resources measurement focus
- **Fund financial statements** — Financial statements presenting individual funds using the measurement focus and basis appropriate to each fund type
- **Major fund** — A fund whose financial significance meets the 10%/5% thresholds, reported in a separate column
- **Net pension liability** — Total pension liability minus plan fiduciary net position, reported on the government-wide Statement of Net Position
- **Deferred outflows of resources** — Items that will be recognized as expense in future periods, reported between assets and liabilities on the Statement of Net Position
