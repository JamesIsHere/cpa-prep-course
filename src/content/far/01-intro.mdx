# Introduction to Financial Accounting and Reporting

## Learning Objectives

- Understand the purpose and scope of the FAR section of the CPA exam
- Identify the major content areas tested in financial accounting
- Distinguish between the key accounting frameworks (GAAP, IFRS, governmental)
- Recognize the conceptual foundation underlying financial reporting

## What is Financial Accounting and Reporting?

Financial Accounting and Reporting is the backbone of the CPA profession. The FAR section tests your ability to prepare, analyze, and report financial information in accordance with established standards. It is widely considered the most content-heavy section of the CPA exam.

FAR covers financial reporting for **commercial entities, not-for-profit organizations, and governmental bodies** — each with its own set of standards and presentation requirements.

## Key Areas of the FAR Section

The FAR section is organized around four content areas:

1. **Financial Reporting** — Preparation and presentation of financial statements, including the balance sheet, income statement, statement of cash flows, and statement of changes in equity.

2. **Select Transactions** — Accounting for specific transaction types such as inventory, fixed assets, leases, revenue recognition, equity, and debt instruments.

3. **Select Financial Statement Accounts** — Detailed rules for individual accounts including receivables, investments, intangible assets, and contingencies.

4. **State and Local Governments** — Governmental accounting using the GASB framework, including fund accounting, government-wide statements, and budgetary reporting.

## Accounting Frameworks

The CPA exam expects you to know multiple frameworks:

- **U.S. GAAP** — The primary framework, issued by the Financial Accounting Standards Board (FASB), used by most commercial entities in the United States.
- **IFRS** — International Financial Reporting Standards, issued by the IASB. The exam tests key differences between GAAP and IFRS.
- **GASB** — Governmental Accounting Standards Board standards for state and local governments.
- **Special Purpose Frameworks** — Cash basis, tax basis, and other non-GAAP frameworks for smaller entities.

## The Conceptual Framework

All of U.S. GAAP is built on the FASB Conceptual Framework, which establishes:

- **Qualitative characteristics** — Relevance and faithful representation as the fundamental qualities of useful financial information
- **Elements of financial statements** — Assets, liabilities, equity, revenues, expenses, gains, and losses
- **Recognition and measurement** — When and at what amount items should be recorded
- **Assumptions** — Going concern, economic entity, monetary unit, and periodicity

> The conceptual framework questions on the exam tend to be straightforward but frequently tested. Knowing the hierarchy — relevance, faithful representation, comparability, verifiability, timeliness, understandability — is essential.

## Key Terms

- **Accrual basis** — Revenues and expenses are recognized when earned or incurred, not when cash changes hands
- **Fair value** — The price at which an asset could be sold or a liability settled in an orderly transaction between market participants
- **Materiality** — Information is material if its omission or misstatement could influence economic decisions of users
- **Going concern** — The assumption that an entity will continue operating for the foreseeable future
