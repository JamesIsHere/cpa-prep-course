# Payables and Accrued Liabilities

## Learning Objectives

- Understand the recognition and measurement of accounts payable and trade payables
- Account for common accrued liabilities including wages, interest, and taxes
- Apply ASC 710 to compensated absences (vacation, sick leave)
- Distinguish between current and noncurrent liability classification

## Accounts Payable

**Accounts payable (trade payables)** represent obligations to pay suppliers for goods or services received in the normal course of business. They are recognized when the entity has received the goods or services and the obligation to pay is established.

Key recognition and measurement rules:

- **Recognition** — Record when title passes to the buyer or when services are received, consistent with the shipping terms (FOB shipping point vs. FOB destination)
- **Measurement** — At the invoice amount, net of any available trade discounts
- **Trade discounts** — Under the **gross method**, payables are recorded at the full invoice amount and discounts are recognized when taken. Under the **net method**, payables are recorded net of the discount and interest expense is recognized if the discount lapses.
- **Cutoff** — Proper period-end cutoff requires matching goods in transit to the appropriate period based on shipping terms. Goods shipped FOB shipping point belong to the buyer; FOB destination belong to the seller until delivery.

> On the CPA exam, accounts payable cutoff questions are common. Always check the shipping terms and whether the goods were in transit at year-end to determine whether the payable (and related inventory) should be recorded.

## Accrued Liabilities

**Accrued liabilities** arise when an expense has been incurred but not yet paid. Under the accrual basis, these must be recognized in the period the expense is incurred, regardless of when cash is paid.

### Common Accrued Liabilities

| Liability | Recognition Trigger | Typical Adjusting Entry |
|-----------|-------------------|------------------------|
| Wages and salaries | Services performed by employees | Debit Wage Expense, Credit Wages Payable |
| Interest payable | Passage of time on outstanding debt | Debit Interest Expense, Credit Interest Payable |
| Property taxes | Passage of time during the fiscal period | Debit Property Tax Expense, Credit Property Tax Payable |
| Utilities payable | Consumption of utilities during the period | Debit Utilities Expense, Credit Utilities Payable |
| Income taxes payable | Taxable income earned during the period | Debit Income Tax Expense, Credit Income Taxes Payable |

### Interest Payable — Worked Example

A company has a $500,000 note payable at 6% annual interest, dated October 1. At December 31 (fiscal year-end), three months of interest have accrued:

Interest = $500,000 × 6% × 3/12 = **$7,500**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|-------|--------|
| Interest Expense | $7,500 | |
| Interest Payable | | $7,500 |

## Compensated Absences — ASC 710

**ASC 710-10, Compensated Absences**, addresses when to accrue liabilities for future employee absences such as vacation, holidays, illness, and other paid time off.

### Accrual Criteria

An employer must accrue a liability for compensated absences when **all four** conditions are met:

1. The obligation relates to services **already rendered** by employees
2. The rights **vest** or **accumulate** (carry forward to future periods)
3. Payment is **probable**
4. The amount can be **reasonably estimated**

**Vesting** means the employer must pay the employee for unused time upon termination. **Accumulating** means unused time carries forward but is not paid upon termination. Both trigger accrual if the other conditions are met.

### Sick Pay Exception

Sick pay that **accumulates but does not vest** need not be accrued (although accrual is permitted). The rationale is that sick pay typically requires an illness to trigger payment, making it less certain. However, if sick pay vests (paid upon termination), it must be accrued.

### Worked Example — Vacation Accrual

An employee earns 15 vacation days per year (equivalent to $200/day) and has used 10 days by December 31. The unused balance of 5 days carries forward (accumulates and vests).

Accrual = 5 days × $200 = **$1,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|-------|--------|
| Compensation Expense | $1,000 | |
| Accrued Compensated Absences | | $1,000 |

## Advance Payments and Deposits

**Customer advances** and **refundable deposits** are recognized as current liabilities when received. They represent an obligation to deliver goods, perform services, or return the deposit.

- **Customer advances** — Debit Cash, Credit Unearned Revenue (or Customer Deposits). Revenue is recognized when the performance obligation is satisfied under ASC 606.
- **Refundable deposits** — Reported as a liability until the deposit conditions are met (e.g., return of rented equipment). If the entity expects to retain the deposit (e.g., a nonrefundable application fee), it is recognized as revenue when received.

## Current vs. Noncurrent Classification

Liabilities are classified as **current** if they are due within one year (or the operating cycle, whichever is longer). Key classification rules:

- **Refinancing** — A short-term obligation may be classified as noncurrent if the entity has both the **intent and ability** to refinance it on a long-term basis. Ability is demonstrated by either (a) actually refinancing before the financial statements are issued, or (b) having an existing noncancelable financing agreement.
- **Callable debt** — If a lender can demand payment due to a covenant violation, the debt is classified as current even if the lender has not called it, unless the lender waives the right for at least 12 months after the balance sheet date.
- **Demand notes** — Always classified as current, regardless of whether the lender is expected to demand payment.

> Liability classification questions on the CPA exam often test the refinancing exception and the effect of covenant violations. Remember: intent alone is not sufficient — the entity must also demonstrate ability to refinance.

## Key Terms

- **Accounts payable** — Obligations to pay suppliers for goods or services received in the ordinary course of business
- **Accrued liability** — An expense that has been incurred but not yet paid, recognized through an adjusting entry
- **Compensated absences** — Paid time off (vacation, sick leave, holidays) that may vest or accumulate under employer policies
- **Vesting** — An employee's right to be paid for unused compensated absences upon termination of employment
- **Unearned revenue** — A liability representing cash received before the related performance obligation has been satisfied
- **Current liability** — An obligation expected to be settled within one year or the operating cycle, whichever is longer
