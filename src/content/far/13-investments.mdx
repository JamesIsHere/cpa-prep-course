# Investments

## Learning Objectives

- Identify investments that are measured at fair value versus amortized cost
- Calculate the carrying amount and investment income for fair value investments
- Calculate impairment losses on investments measured at fair value and at amortized cost
- Apply the equity method of accounting for investments with significant influence
- Prepare journal entries for acquisitions, income recognition, dividends, and disposals under each method

## Investment Classification Overview

The accounting for investments depends on the **type of instrument** and the **investor's intent and influence**. The AICPA Blueprint organizes this into three categories:

1. **Financial assets at fair value** — equity securities and certain debt securities
2. **Financial assets at amortized cost** — held-to-maturity debt securities
3. **Equity method investments** — significant influence (typically 20-50% ownership)

## Financial Assets at Fair Value

### Equity Securities (ASC 321)

Under ASC 321, **all equity securities with readily determinable fair values** are measured at fair value through net income. There is no more "available-for-sale" classification for equity securities.

**Key rules:**
- Measure at **fair value** each reporting period
- Unrealized gains and losses are recognized in **net income** (not OCI)
- No impairment model — changes in fair value flow through earnings automatically

### Equity Securities Without Readily Determinable Fair Values

An entity may elect a **measurement alternative**: report at cost minus impairment, adjusted for observable price changes in orderly transactions for identical or similar investments.

### Debt Securities — Trading

Trading debt securities are held for **short-term resale**:
- Measured at **fair value** each period
- Unrealized gains and losses in **net income**
- Interest income recognized separately

### Debt Securities — Available-for-Sale (AFS)

AFS debt securities are neither trading nor held-to-maturity:
- Measured at **fair value** each period
- Unrealized gains and losses in **other comprehensive income (OCI)**
- Reclassified to net income when sold
- Subject to the **CECL impairment model** for credit losses

**Impairment of AFS debt securities:**

When fair value drops below amortized cost:
1. Determine if any portion of the decline is due to **credit loss** (expected cash flow shortfall)
2. Credit losses → recognized in net income through an **allowance** (reversible)
3. Non-credit portion → remains in OCI

## Financial Assets at Amortized Cost

### Held-to-Maturity (HTM) Debt Securities

Debt securities that the entity has the **positive intent and ability** to hold until maturity:
- Measured at **amortized cost** (face value adjusted for premium/discount)
- Interest income recognized using the **effective interest method**
- No fair value adjustments on the balance sheet
- Subject to the **CECL impairment model**

**Journal entry — Purchase at a premium:**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|-------|--------|
| Investment in HTM Securities | XXX | |
| Cash | | XXX |

**Journal entry — Interest receipt and premium amortization:**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|-------|--------|
| Cash | XXX | |
| Investment in HTM Securities | | XXX |
| Interest Income | | XXX |

Cash received = face value × stated rate. Interest income = carrying amount × market rate. The difference amortizes the premium.

**CECL impairment for HTM:**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|-------|--------|
| Credit Loss Expense | XXX | |
| Allowance for Credit Losses — HTM | | XXX |

Unlike AFS, the entire fair value decline is not analyzed — only expected credit losses are recognized.

## Equity Method Investments (ASC 323)

### When to Apply

The equity method applies when the investor has **significant influence** over the investee — generally presumed at **20% to 50% ownership** of voting stock. Indicators of significant influence include:

- Board representation
- Participation in policy-making
- Material intercompany transactions
- Interchange of managerial personnel
- Technological dependency

### Initial Recognition

Record at **cost** (purchase price plus direct acquisition costs):

### Recognizing Investee Income

The investor records its **proportionate share** of the investee's net income:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|-------|--------|
| Investment in Equity Method Investee | XXX | |
| Equity in Earnings of Investee | | XXX |

This is a **single-line consolidation** — the investor picks up its share of earnings without consolidating individual assets and liabilities.

### Dividends Received

Dividends **reduce the investment balance** (they are a return of investment, not income):

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|-------|--------|
| Cash | XXX | |
| Investment in Equity Method Investee | | XXX |

### Carrying Amount Formula

**Ending Balance = Cost + Share of Income − Dividends Received − Impairment**

### Impairment

Equity method investments are evaluated for impairment when events indicate the carrying amount may not be recoverable. If the decline in value is **other than temporary**, write down to fair value:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|-------|--------|
| Loss on Impairment of Investment | XXX | |
| Investment in Equity Method Investee | | XXX |

### Basis Differences

When the investor pays more than book value for the investee's net assets, the excess is allocated to:
1. **Identifiable assets** (e.g., undervalued land, equipment) — amortized over useful life
2. **Goodwill** — not amortized, but considered in impairment analysis

The amortization of basis differences **reduces** the investor's equity in earnings each period.

## Summary Comparison

| Feature | Fair Value (Equity) | Fair Value (AFS Debt) | Amortized Cost (HTM) | Equity Method |
|---------|--------------------|-----------------------|----------------------|---------------|
| Measurement | Fair value | Fair value | Amortized cost | Cost + share of earnings |
| Unrealized G/L | Net income | OCI | N/A | N/A |
| Income recognition | Dividends | Interest (effective rate) | Interest (effective rate) | Share of net income |
| Impairment | N/A (FV through NI) | CECL (credit portion) | CECL | Other-than-temporary |

## Key Exam Tips

1. **Equity securities always go through net income** — there is no more AFS classification for stocks under ASC 321
2. **AFS debt impairment splits credit vs. non-credit** — credit losses hit income, non-credit stays in OCI
3. **HTM requires intent AND ability** — if either is lacking, reclassify to AFS
4. **Equity method dividends reduce the investment** — they are not income
5. **Basis differences must be amortized** — they reduce equity in earnings each period
