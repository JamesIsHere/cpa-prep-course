# Employee Benefit Plans

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the components of net periodic pension cost for defined benefit plans under ASC 715
- Distinguish between PBO, ABO, and VBO and calculate the funded status
- Account for prior service cost, gains and losses, and the corridor approach
- Record defined contribution plan expense and employer matching contributions
- Apply OPEB accounting for postretirement benefits other than pensions under ASC 715-60

## Defined Benefit Plans — Overview

A **defined benefit plan** promises employees a specified monthly benefit upon retirement, typically based on a formula involving salary and years of service. The employer bears the investment risk and the obligation to fund the promised benefits. Accounting for defined benefit plans is governed by **ASC 715, Compensation — Retirement Benefits**.

The central accounting question is: how much pension expense should the employer recognize each period, and what is the funded status reported on the balance sheet?

### Benefit Obligation Measures

Three measures of the pension obligation exist, each reflecting a different level of assumptions:

| Measure | Definition | Includes Future Salary Increases? |
|---------|-----------|-----------------------------------|
| **Vested Benefit Obligation (VBO)** | Benefits earned by employees who have met vesting requirements | No |
| **Accumulated Benefit Obligation (ABO)** | Benefits earned to date based on current salary levels | No |
| **Projected Benefit Obligation (PBO)** | Benefits earned to date based on estimated future salary levels | Yes |

The **PBO** is the measure used for balance sheet and expense calculations under ASC 715. It is always equal to or greater than the ABO because it incorporates projected salary increases.

## Net Periodic Pension Cost

Net periodic pension cost (pension expense) has five components:

### 1. Service Cost

The present value of benefits earned by employees during the **current period**. This is the only component presented as an operating expense on the income statement. All other components are reported below operating income (typically in other income/expense).

### 2. Interest Cost

Interest accruing on the beginning PBO at the **discount rate** (high-quality corporate bond rate matching the expected benefit payment timing).

**Interest Cost = Beginning PBO x Discount Rate**

### 3. Expected Return on Plan Assets

The expected long-term rate of return applied to the **beginning fair value of plan assets**. This amount is **subtracted** from pension expense because earnings on plan assets offset the cost of providing benefits.

**Expected Return = Beginning Plan Assets x Expected Rate of Return**

### 4. Amortization of Prior Service Cost

When a plan is amended to increase (or decrease) benefits retroactively, the resulting **prior service cost** is not expensed immediately. It is recognized in **other comprehensive income (OCI)** and then amortized to pension expense over the **average remaining service period** of employees expected to receive benefits.

### 5. Amortization of Net Gain or Loss (Corridor Approach)

Actuarial gains and losses arise from changes in assumptions (discount rate, mortality, turnover) and differences between expected and actual returns on plan assets. These are initially recognized in OCI.

Amortization to pension expense is required only when the **cumulative unrecognized net gain or loss** exceeds a **corridor** equal to the greater of:

- 10% of the beginning PBO, or
- 10% of the beginning fair value of plan assets

The amount amortized equals the **excess over the corridor** divided by the average remaining service period of active employees.

### Pension Expense Formula

**Net Periodic Pension Cost = Service Cost + Interest Cost - Expected Return on Plan Assets + Amortization of Prior Service Cost + Amortization of Net Loss (or - Net Gain)**

### Worked Example — Net Periodic Pension Cost

Greystone Inc. has the following pension data for the current year:

- Beginning PBO: $2,000,000
- Beginning plan assets (fair value): $1,800,000
- Service cost: $150,000
- Discount rate: 5%
- Expected rate of return on plan assets: 7%
- Amortization of prior service cost: $10,000
- Cumulative unrecognized net loss: $250,000
- Average remaining service period: 10 years

**Step 1: Interest cost** = $2,000,000 x 5% = **$100,000**

**Step 2: Expected return** = $1,800,000 x 7% = **$126,000**

**Step 3: Corridor test**
- 10% of beginning PBO: $200,000
- 10% of beginning plan assets: $180,000
- Corridor: $200,000 (the greater amount)
- Excess: $250,000 - $200,000 = $50,000
- Amortization: $50,000 / 10 = **$5,000**

**Net Periodic Pension Cost:**
$150,000 + $100,000 - $126,000 + $10,000 + $5,000 = **$139,000**

## Funded Status on the Balance Sheet

ASC 715 requires the **funded status** of the plan to be reported on the balance sheet:

**Funded Status = Fair Value of Plan Assets - PBO**

- If plan assets exceed the PBO → **Net pension asset** (noncurrent asset)
- If the PBO exceeds plan assets → **Net pension liability** (typically noncurrent; current portion if benefits payable within 12 months exceed plan assets)

Using the Greystone example above: Funded status = $1,800,000 - $2,000,000 = **($200,000)** — reported as a net pension liability of $200,000.

### Items Recognized in OCI

The following pension-related items are reported in **other comprehensive income** and accumulated in AOCI on the balance sheet:

- **Prior service cost** (unamortized portion from plan amendments)
- **Net actuarial gain or loss** (unamortized portion from assumption changes and experience differences)
- **Net transition obligation or asset** (from initial adoption of ASC 715, if any remains)

These items are reclassified from OCI to pension expense as they are amortized.

## Defined Contribution Plans

A **defined contribution plan** (e.g., 401(k)) specifies the employer's contribution to individual employee accounts. The employee bears the investment risk — the employer's obligation is limited to making the agreed contributions.

Accounting is straightforward:

**Pension Expense = Employer's Required Contribution for the Period**

If the employer has not yet funded the contribution, a liability is recognized. If contributions exceed the required amount, a prepaid asset is recorded. There is no PBO, no plan asset accounting, no corridor calculation, and no OCI items.

### Employer Matching

Many plans include a **matching contribution** — the employer matches a portion of the employee's voluntary contributions. For example, a company might match 50% of employee contributions up to 6% of salary. The matching contribution is expensed as earned by employees (typically each pay period).

**Example:** An employee earns $80,000 and contributes 6% ($4,800). The employer matches 50%, so employer expense = $4,800 x 50% = **$2,400** for the year.

## OPEB — Other Postretirement Employee Benefits (ASC 715-60)

**Other postretirement employee benefits (OPEB)** include healthcare, life insurance, and other nonpension benefits promised to retired employees. OPEB accounting under **ASC 715-60** parallels pension accounting in structure but has important differences.

### Key OPEB Concepts

**Accumulated Postretirement Benefit Obligation (APBO)** — The actuarial present value of all future postretirement benefits attributed to employee service to date. This is analogous to the PBO in pension accounting.

**Expected Postretirement Benefit Obligation (EPBO)** — The total expected cost of providing postretirement benefits, both past and future service. The APBO is the portion of the EPBO attributed to service already rendered.

### OPEB Expense Components

OPEB expense follows the same structure as pension expense:

1. **Service cost** — Benefits attributed to the current year
2. **Interest cost** — Discount rate applied to the beginning APBO
3. **Expected return on plan assets** — Subtracted (many OPEB plans are unfunded or underfunded, so this may be zero)
4. **Amortization of prior service cost** — From plan amendments
5. **Amortization of net gain/loss** — Same corridor approach as pensions

### Key Differences: Pensions vs. OPEB

| Feature | Defined Benefit Pension | OPEB |
|---------|------------------------|------|
| Primary obligation measure | PBO | APBO |
| Typically funded? | Yes (ERISA requirements) | Often unfunded or partially funded |
| Healthcare cost trend rate | N/A | Required assumption affecting APBO |
| Full eligibility date | Retirement date | Date employee achieves full eligibility (may be before retirement) |
| Attribution period | Hire date to retirement | Hire date to full eligibility date |

> On the CPA exam, OPEB questions often focus on the healthcare cost trend rate (the expected annual rate of increase in per-capita healthcare costs) and the fact that many OPEB plans are unfunded, resulting in large liabilities on the balance sheet.

## Curtailments and Settlements

A **curtailment** occurs when an event significantly reduces the expected years of future service or eliminates the accrual of benefits for a significant number of employees (e.g., plant closing, workforce reduction). The effect on the PBO is recognized immediately as a gain or loss.

A **settlement** occurs when the employer irrevocably relieves itself of the pension obligation (e.g., lump-sum payment to employees, purchasing annuity contracts). A settlement gain or loss is recognized when the obligation is settled.

## Key Terms

- **Projected Benefit Obligation (PBO)** — Present value of pension benefits earned to date, incorporating projected salary increases
- **Accumulated Benefit Obligation (ABO)** — Present value of benefits earned to date using current salary levels
- **Funded status** — Fair value of plan assets minus the PBO; reported on the balance sheet
- **Service cost** — The present value of benefits earned during the current period
- **Discount rate** — High-quality corporate bond rate used to calculate interest cost and present value of the obligation
- **Expected return on plan assets** — Long-term rate of return applied to plan assets, subtracted from pension expense
- **Prior service cost** — Cost from retroactive plan amendments, recognized in OCI and amortized over remaining service period
- **Corridor** — 10% of the greater of beginning PBO or beginning plan assets; triggers amortization of net gains/losses
- **APBO** — Accumulated Postretirement Benefit Obligation, the OPEB equivalent of the PBO
- **Healthcare cost trend rate** — The expected annual rate of increase in per-capita healthcare costs used in OPEB valuations
- **Curtailment** — An event that significantly reduces future service or eliminates benefit accrual
- **Settlement** — Irrevocable discharge of a pension obligation through lump-sum payment or annuity purchase
