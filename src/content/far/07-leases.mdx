# Leases

## Learning Objectives

- Distinguish between operating leases and finance leases under ASC 842
- Account for right-of-use assets and lease liabilities on the lessee's balance sheet
- Apply the lessor's classification criteria and accounting models
- Calculate lease payments and present value for lease recognition

## Overview of ASC 842

**ASC 842** fundamentally changed lease accounting by requiring lessees to recognize virtually all leases on the balance sheet. Under the prior standard (ASC 840), operating leases were off-balance-sheet. Now, both operating and finance leases result in a **right-of-use (ROU) asset** and a **lease liability** on the lessee's balance sheet.

The only exception is **short-term leases** — leases with a term of 12 months or less (with no purchase option the lessee is reasonably certain to exercise). Entities may elect to exclude these from balance sheet recognition and instead recognize lease payments on a straight-line basis.

## Lessee Accounting

### Lease Classification

A lessee classifies a lease as a **finance lease** if it meets any one of the following criteria:

1. The lease transfers **ownership** of the underlying asset to the lessee by the end of the term
2. The lease grants the lessee a **purchase option** that the lessee is reasonably certain to exercise
3. The lease term is for the **major part** of the remaining economic life of the asset (often interpreted as 75% or more)
4. The present value of lease payments equals or exceeds **substantially all** of the fair value of the asset (often interpreted as 90% or more)
5. The underlying asset is so **specialized** that it is expected to have no alternative use to the lessor at the end of the lease term

If none of these criteria are met, the lease is an **operating lease**.

> The 75% and 90% thresholds are bright-line tests from the old standard (ASC 840). Under ASC 842, these are not explicitly stated but remain common in practice as reasonable benchmarks. The exam may reference them.

### Initial Measurement

At lease commencement, the lessee records:

- **Lease liability** = Present value of future lease payments, discounted at the rate implicit in the lease (if determinable) or the lessee's incremental borrowing rate
- **ROU asset** = Lease liability + Initial direct costs + Prepaid lease payments - Lease incentives received

### Subsequent Measurement — Finance Lease

- The **lease liability** is reduced as payments are made, with interest expense recognized using the effective interest method
- The **ROU asset** is amortized on a straight-line basis (typically) over the shorter of the lease term or the useful life of the asset
- Total expense is **front-loaded** because interest is higher in early periods while amortization is level

### Subsequent Measurement — Operating Lease

- A single **lease expense** is recognized on a straight-line basis over the lease term
- The lease liability is reduced using the effective interest method
- The ROU asset is adjusted as the balancing figure (lease expense minus interest on the liability)
- Total expense is **level** each period

### Worked Example

A company enters a 5-year operating lease with annual payments of $10,000, paid at the end of each year. The lessee's incremental borrowing rate is 6%. There are no initial direct costs, prepayments, or incentives.

**Step 1 — Calculate the lease liability (PV of an ordinary annuity):**

PV = $10,000 x [(1 - (1 + 0.06)^-5) / 0.06] = $10,000 x 4.2124 = **$42,124**

**Step 2 — Record the ROU asset:**

ROU asset = $42,124 (equal to the lease liability with no adjustments)

**Step 3 — Annual lease expense (operating lease):**

Straight-line expense = $10,000 x 5 years / 5 = **$10,000 per year**

**Year 1 journal entries:**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|-------|--------|
| Lease expense | $10,000 | |
| Lease liability | $7,473 | |
| ROU asset | | $7,473 |
| Cash | | $10,000 |

Interest on the liability in Year 1 = $42,124 x 6% = $2,527. The liability reduction is $10,000 - $2,527 = $7,473.

## Lessor Accounting

Lessor accounting under ASC 842 remained largely consistent with the prior standard. A lessor classifies leases into three categories:

### Sales-Type Lease

Meets any of the five finance lease criteria. At commencement:
- Derecognize the underlying asset
- Recognize a **net investment in the lease** = PV of lease payments + PV of unguaranteed residual
- Recognize **selling profit or loss** = Net investment - Carrying amount of asset
- Subsequently, recognize interest income using the effective interest method on the net investment

The net investment is composed of the **lease receivable** (PV of lease payments) and the **unguaranteed residual asset** (PV of the residual the lessor expects to recover).

### Direct Financing Lease

Does not meet any of the five criteria, but the present value of lease payments plus the present value of the residual value (guaranteed or unguaranteed) equals **substantially all** of the asset's fair value, and collection is probable. At commencement:
- Derecognize the underlying asset
- Recognize a net investment in the lease
- **Defer** any selling profit (it is included in the net investment and recognized over the lease term as part of interest income)
- Selling losses, however, are recognized immediately

### Operating Lease

Does not qualify as sales-type or direct financing. The lessor:
- Keeps the asset on its books and continues to depreciate it
- Recognizes lease income on a **straight-line basis** over the lease term
- Initial direct costs are capitalized and amortized over the lease term

| Feature | Sales-Type | Direct Financing | Operating |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|-----------|
| Asset derecognized | Yes | Yes | No |
| Selling profit | Recognized immediately | Deferred | N/A |
| Selling loss | Recognized immediately | Recognized immediately | N/A |
| Income pattern | Interest income (front-loaded) | Interest income (front-loaded) | Straight-line |

## Lease Modifications

A **lease modification** is a change in the scope or consideration of a lease that was not part of the original terms. The accounting depends on whether the modification grants an additional right of use:

**Treated as a separate lease** — When the modification grants an **additional right of use** that is distinct and the lease payments increase commensurate with the standalone price. Account for the new lease independently.

**Not a separate lease** — When the modification does not qualify as a separate lease:
- **Lessee:** Remeasure the lease liability using a revised discount rate, and adjust the ROU asset accordingly. If the modification decreases the scope, reduce the ROU asset proportionally and recognize any difference as a gain or loss.
- **Lessor:** Reassess classification of the modified lease. If the classification changes, account for the modified lease as a new lease from the modification date.

## Subleases

When a lessee (the intermediate lessor) subleases a right-of-use asset to a third party:
- The **original lease** between the lessee and the head lessor continues unchanged
- The lessee classifies the **sublease** as a lessor, using the same sales-type/direct financing/operating criteria
- The classification of the sublease is based on the **right-of-use asset** (not the underlying asset), which means the relevant economic life is the remaining lease term of the head lease
- The original ROU asset remains on the lessee's books unless the sublease is classified as sales-type or direct financing

## Sale-Leaseback Transactions

In a **sale-leaseback**, an entity sells an asset and immediately leases it back from the buyer. Under ASC 842, the transaction is analyzed in two steps:

**Step 1 — Does the transfer qualify as a sale under ASC 606?** The seller-lessee must transfer control of the asset to the buyer-lessor. If the leaseback is a finance lease or if the seller-lessee has a repurchase option, control has not transferred and the transaction **fails** sale treatment.

**If the transfer is NOT a sale:**
- The seller-lessee does not derecognize the asset
- The cash received is recorded as a **financing obligation** (a loan)
- The buyer-lessor does not recognize the asset; it records a receivable

**If the transfer IS a sale:**
- The seller-lessee derecognizes the asset and recognizes the ROU asset and lease liability for the leaseback
- Any gain or loss on the sale is recognized only to the extent it relates to the **rights transferred** to the buyer (the portion not leased back). Gain on the retained right (the leaseback portion) is deferred as an adjustment to the ROU asset.

> Sale-leaseback questions test whether the transfer qualifies as a sale and how to allocate the gain. Remember: if the leaseback is a finance lease, it is not a sale — it is a financing arrangement.

## Key Terms

- **Right-of-use (ROU) asset** — An asset representing the lessee's right to use an underlying asset for the lease term
- **Lease liability** — The lessee's obligation to make lease payments, measured at present value
- **Incremental borrowing rate** — The rate the lessee would pay to borrow funds on a collateralized basis over a similar term
- **Finance lease** — A lease that effectively transfers the risks and rewards of ownership to the lessee; results in front-loaded expense
- **Short-term lease** — A lease with a term of 12 months or less, eligible for an election to exclude from balance sheet recognition

## Practice Problems

**1.** Under ASC 842, which of the following is true for an operating lease on the lessee's balance sheet?

a) Only a lease liability is recognized; no asset is recorded
b) Both a right-of-use asset and a lease liability are recognized
c) Neither an asset nor a liability is recognized
d) The underlying asset is recorded as a purchased asset

**Answer:** b) Both a right-of-use asset and a lease liability are recognized — ASC 842 requires lessees to recognize both an ROU asset and a lease liability for virtually all leases, including operating leases.

---

**2.** A lessee enters a 4-year lease with annual payments of $20,000. The incremental borrowing rate is 5%. The present value factor for an ordinary annuity at 5% for 4 years is 3.5460. What is the initial lease liability?

a) $80,000
b) $70,920
c) $76,000
d) $65,460

**Answer:** b) $70,920 — Lease liability = $20,000 x 3.5460 = $70,920.

---

**3.** How does expense recognition differ between a finance lease and an operating lease for a lessee?

a) Finance leases have level total expense; operating leases have front-loaded expense
b) Finance leases have front-loaded total expense; operating leases have level total expense
c) Both have level total expense over the lease term
d) Both have front-loaded total expense over the lease term

**Answer:** b) Finance leases have front-loaded total expense; operating leases have level total expense — finance leases combine declining interest with level amortization, resulting in higher total expense in early years. Operating leases recognize a single straight-line expense.
