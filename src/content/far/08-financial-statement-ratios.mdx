# Financial Statement Ratios and Performance Metrics

## Learning Objectives

- Calculate and interpret profitability ratios including gross profit margin, return on assets, and return on equity
- Calculate and interpret liquidity ratios including current ratio, quick ratio, and turnover ratios
- Calculate and interpret solvency ratios including debt-to-equity and times interest earned
- Compute performance metrics such as EBITDA, price-to-earnings, and dividend payout ratios
- Analyze variances between budget and actual results

## Why Ratios Matter on the CPA Exam

The AICPA Blueprint explicitly tests your ability to **identify, calculate, and apply** financial statement ratios. This isn't just about memorizing formulas — the exam expects you to know *which* ratio answers a given analytical question, compute it correctly, and interpret what it means for the entity's financial health.

Ratios fall into four categories: **profitability, liquidity, solvency, and performance metrics.**

## Profitability Ratios

Profitability ratios measure how effectively an entity generates earnings relative to revenue, assets, or equity.

### Gross Profit Margin

**Gross Profit Margin = (Revenue − Cost of Goods Sold) / Revenue**

Measures the percentage of revenue retained after direct costs. A declining gross margin signals rising input costs or pricing pressure.

### Return on Sales (Net Profit Margin)

**Return on Sales = Net Income / Revenue**

Shows how much of each dollar of revenue flows to the bottom line after all expenses.

### Return on Assets (ROA)

**ROA = Net Income / Average Total Assets**

Measures how efficiently management uses assets to generate profit. Use **average** total assets (beginning + ending ÷ 2) unless the problem specifies otherwise.

### Return on Equity (ROE)

**ROE = Net Income / Average Stockholders' Equity**

Measures the return generated on shareholders' investment. ROE can be decomposed using the **DuPont formula**:

**ROE = Net Profit Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier**

## Liquidity Ratios

Liquidity ratios assess an entity's ability to meet short-term obligations.

### Current Ratio

**Current Ratio = Current Assets / Current Liabilities**

A ratio above 1.0 indicates more current assets than current liabilities. However, a very high current ratio may signal inefficient use of assets.

### Quick Ratio (Acid-Test)

**Quick Ratio = (Cash + Short-Term Investments + Net Receivables) / Current Liabilities**

Excludes inventory and prepaid expenses — assets that may not convert to cash quickly.

### Accounts Receivable Turnover

**AR Turnover = Net Credit Sales / Average Accounts Receivable**

**Days Sales Outstanding = 365 / AR Turnover**

Higher turnover means faster collection. Days sales outstanding (DSO) converts the ratio to average collection period.

### Inventory Turnover

**Inventory Turnover = Cost of Goods Sold / Average Inventory**

**Days Inventory Outstanding = 365 / Inventory Turnover**

### Accounts Payable Turnover

**AP Turnover = Cost of Goods Sold / Average Accounts Payable**

**Days Payable Outstanding = 365 / AP Turnover**

### Cash Conversion Cycle

**CCC = DSO + DIO − DPO**

The cash conversion cycle combines receivable, inventory, and payable turnover into a single measure of how long cash is tied up in operating activities.

## Solvency Ratios

Solvency ratios evaluate an entity's ability to meet long-term obligations and its capital structure.

### Debt-to-Equity Ratio

**Debt-to-Equity = Total Liabilities / Total Stockholders' Equity**

Higher values indicate greater financial leverage and risk.

### Total Debt Ratio

**Total Debt Ratio = Total Liabilities / Total Assets**

Shows the proportion of assets financed by debt rather than equity.

### Times Interest Earned (Interest Coverage)

**TIE = Earnings Before Interest and Taxes (EBIT) / Interest Expense**

Measures how comfortably the entity can cover its interest payments from operating earnings. A ratio below 1.5 is generally considered a warning sign.

## Performance Metrics

### EBITDA

**EBITDA = Net Income + Interest + Taxes + Depreciation + Amortization**

A proxy for operating cash flow, widely used in valuation and debt covenants. Note: EBITDA is not a GAAP measure — the exam may test your understanding of its limitations.

### Price-to-Earnings (P/E) Ratio

**P/E = Market Price per Share / Earnings per Share**

Measures how much investors are willing to pay per dollar of earnings.

### Dividend Payout Ratio

**Dividend Payout = Dividends per Share / Earnings per Share**

Shows the percentage of earnings distributed to shareholders versus retained.

### Asset Turnover

**Asset Turnover = Net Revenue / Average Total Assets**

Measures how efficiently the entity uses its assets to generate revenue.

## Budget-to-Actual Variance Analysis

The CPA exam tests your ability to calculate and interpret variances between budgeted and actual results.

### Favorable vs. Unfavorable Variances

- **Favorable variance**: Actual revenue exceeds budget, or actual expense is less than budget
- **Unfavorable variance**: Actual revenue is below budget, or actual expense exceeds budget

### Calculating Variances

**Variance = Actual − Budget**

For revenue items, a positive variance is favorable. For expense items, a negative variance (actual < budget) is favorable.

### Variance Percentage

**Variance % = ((Actual − Budget) / Budget) × 100**

Variance analysis is most meaningful when you identify the **cause** — volume changes, price changes, or efficiency changes — not just the dollar amount.

## Key Exam Tips

1. **Always use averages** for balance sheet items in ratio denominators unless told otherwise
2. **Know which ratio answers which question** — the exam often describes a scenario and asks you to select the appropriate ratio
3. **Understand directional impact** — if inventory increases, what happens to current ratio? To inventory turnover?
4. **Watch for non-GAAP metrics** — EBITDA is useful but has limitations; the exam may test your awareness of these
5. **Budget variances require context** — a favorable expense variance from cutting maintenance may signal future problems
