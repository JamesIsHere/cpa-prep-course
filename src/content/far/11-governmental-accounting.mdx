# Governmental Accounting

## Learning Objectives

- Understand the GASB framework and how it differs from FASB standards
- Identify the three categories of fund types and their measurement focuses
- Distinguish between government-wide and fund financial statements
- Apply the modified accrual basis of accounting for governmental funds

## The GASB Framework

State and local governments follow standards issued by the **Governmental Accounting Standards Board (GASB)**, not the FASB. Government accounting exists to meet different objectives than commercial accounting. The primary users of government financial statements are citizens, legislative bodies, oversight agencies, and creditors — stakeholders focused on **accountability** rather than profitability.

The two key objectives of governmental financial reporting are:

1. **Fiscal accountability** — Demonstrating that the government raised and spent resources in compliance with budgets, laws, and regulations
2. **Operational accountability** — Reporting on the efficiency and effectiveness of service delivery and the government's ability to continue providing services

Government financial statements are presented in a **Comprehensive Annual Financial Report (CAFR)**, which includes management's discussion and analysis (MD&A), basic financial statements, required supplementary information, and notes.

## Fund Accounting

Governments use **fund accounting** to track resources that are segregated for specific purposes. A fund is a self-balancing set of accounts. Funds are categorized into three groups:

### Governmental Funds

These account for the government's basic service activities. They use the **modified accrual basis** of accounting and focus on **current financial resources** (what is available to spend in the near term).

- **General Fund** — The primary operating fund; accounts for all resources not required to be in another fund. Every government has exactly one.
- **Special Revenue Funds** — Account for resources restricted or committed to specific purposes other than debt service or capital projects
- **Capital Projects Funds** — Account for acquisition and construction of major capital assets
- **Debt Service Funds** — Account for principal and interest payments on long-term debt
- **Permanent Funds** — Account for resources legally restricted so that only earnings (not principal) may be used for the government's programs

### Proprietary Funds

These account for activities that operate like businesses. They use the **full accrual basis** of accounting and focus on **economic resources** (all assets and liabilities).

- **Enterprise Funds** — For activities that charge fees to external users (water utilities, airports, parking garages)
- **Internal Service Funds** — For activities that provide goods or services to other departments within the government (motor pools, printing services)

### Fiduciary Funds

These account for resources held in trust for parties outside the government. They use the **full accrual basis** and focus on **economic resources**. Fiduciary funds are not included in government-wide statements because the resources do not belong to the government.

- **Pension (and other employee benefit) trust funds**
- **Investment trust funds**
- **Private-purpose trust funds**
- **Custodial funds**

> The General Fund is the most frequently tested fund on the CPA exam. If a question does not specify the fund, it is almost certainly referring to the General Fund.

## Government-Wide vs. Fund Financial Statements

GASB requires **dual-perspective reporting** — two sets of financial statements that provide different views of the government's finances.

### Government-Wide Statements

Present the government as a whole using the **full accrual basis** and **economic resources** measurement focus. They include:

- **Statement of Net Position** — Similar to a balance sheet. Reports assets + deferred outflows - liabilities - deferred inflows = net position. Net position is classified as: net investment in capital assets, restricted, or unrestricted.
- **Statement of Activities** — Reports expenses by function/program, offset by program revenues (charges for services, operating grants, capital grants). The difference is the net expense, which is funded by general revenues (taxes, unrestricted grants).

Government-wide statements separate activities into **governmental activities** and **business-type activities**. Fiduciary funds are excluded.

### Fund Financial Statements

Present individual funds using the measurement focus and basis of accounting appropriate to each fund type:

- Governmental funds: Modified accrual, current financial resources
- Proprietary funds: Full accrual, economic resources
- Fiduciary funds: Full accrual, economic resources

Reconciliations between fund statements and government-wide statements are required to explain the differences (primarily capital assets, long-term debt, and accrual adjustments).

## Modified Accrual Basis

The **modified accrual basis** is unique to governmental funds. It recognizes:

**Revenues** — When they are both **measurable** and **available**. Available means collectible within the current period or soon enough thereafter to pay current liabilities (typically 60 days after year-end).

**Expenditures** — When the related liability is incurred, except for:
- Principal and interest on long-term debt — Recognized when **due** (maturity date)
- Compensated absences, claims and judgments, pensions — Recognized to the extent they will be paid with **current available resources**

Under modified accrual, governmental funds do not report long-term assets or long-term liabilities on their balance sheets. Capital outlays are reported as expenditures (not capitalized), and bond proceeds are reported as **other financing sources** (not liabilities). This is a major difference from full accrual and a frequent exam topic.

## Budgetary Accounting

Many governments are legally required to adopt an annual budget. At the beginning of the fiscal year, the budget is recorded in the General Fund:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|-------|--------|
| Estimated revenues | $XX | |
| Budgetary fund balance | (plug) | |
| Appropriations | | $XX |

If estimated revenues exceed appropriations, the budgetary fund balance is credited (expected surplus). If appropriations exceed estimated revenues, it is debited (expected deficit).

During the year, **encumbrances** are recorded when purchase orders are issued, reserving a portion of the budget. When the goods are received, the encumbrance is reversed and the actual expenditure is recorded.

## GASB 34 — The Reporting Model

**GASB Statement No. 34** established the current financial reporting model for state and local governments, requiring the dual-perspective approach of government-wide and fund financial statements. Key requirements include:

- **Management's Discussion and Analysis (MD&A)** — Required supplementary information providing an analytical overview of financial activities. Presented before the basic financial statements.
- **Government-wide financial statements** — Statement of Net Position and Statement of Activities using full accrual, economic resources focus
- **Fund financial statements** — Separate statements for governmental, proprietary, and fiduciary funds
- **Reconciliations** — Required between government-wide and fund statements
- **Infrastructure assets** — Governments must report major infrastructure assets (roads, bridges, water systems). They may use either the **depreciation approach** or the **modified approach** (report maintenance costs rather than depreciation if the government maintains the asset at or above a condition level established by the government).
- **Budgetary comparison** — Required supplementary information comparing original budget, final budget, and actual results for the general fund and major special revenue funds

### Derived Tax Revenue vs. Imposed Tax Revenue

Government revenue recognition follows specific rules:

- **Derived tax revenues** (income tax, sales tax) — Recognized when the underlying exchange occurs (when income is earned, when a sale is made), subject to the availability criterion for governmental funds
- **Imposed nonexchange revenues** (property tax, fines) — Recognized in the period for which they are **levied** or when an enforceable legal claim arises, subject to availability
- **Government-mandated and voluntary nonexchange transactions** (grants) — Recognized when all **eligibility requirements** are met (including time requirements)

## Pension Accounting — GASB 68

**GASB 68** governs pension reporting for state and local government employers that participate in defined benefit pension plans. Key concepts:

### Net Pension Liability (or Asset)

On the government-wide Statement of Net Position, employers report a **net pension liability** (or asset):

**Net Pension Liability = Total Pension Liability − Plan Fiduciary Net Position**

- **Total pension liability (TPL)** — The actuarial present value of projected pension benefits attributed to past service
- **Plan fiduciary net position** — The fair value of assets held in the pension trust

### Pension Expense

Annual pension expense includes:
- **Service cost** — Present value of benefits earned by employees in the current period
- **Interest on total pension liability** — TPL beginning balance x discount rate
- **Changes in benefit terms** — Recognized immediately
- **Projected earnings on plan assets** — Expected investment return (reduces expense)
- **Amortization of deferred outflows/inflows** — Differences between expected and actual experience, and changes in assumptions, are recognized in pension expense over a closed period (typically the average remaining service life of employees)

### Deferred Outflows and Deferred Inflows

Differences between expected and actual results are **not** recognized immediately in pension expense. Instead, they are reported as **deferred outflows of resources** or **deferred inflows of resources** and amortized into expense over future periods:

- Difference between projected and actual earnings on plan assets → amortized over 5 years
- Changes in assumptions and experience differences → amortized over average remaining service life
- Employer contributions made **after** the measurement date → reported as deferred outflow and recognized next period

> GASB 68 pension questions frequently test the net pension liability calculation and the treatment of deferred outflows/inflows. Remember: plan assets are subtracted from the total pension liability — they are not reported separately on the employer's balance sheet.

## OPEB — GASB 75

**GASB 75** mirrors GASB 68 but applies to **other postemployment benefits (OPEB)** — primarily retiree health insurance. The framework is identical:

- Report a **net OPEB liability (or asset)** = Total OPEB liability − Plan fiduciary net position
- OPEB expense includes service cost, interest, and amortization of deferred amounts
- Deferred outflows and inflows are used for experience differences and assumption changes
- The discount rate depends on whether the plan is funded (use expected return on assets) or unfunded (use a high-quality municipal bond index rate)

The main difference from pensions is that many governments have **unfunded** OPEB plans (no trust assets), which means the net OPEB liability equals the full total OPEB liability — often a very large number.

## Key Terms

- **Fund** — A self-balancing set of accounts segregated for a specific purpose, using its own measurement focus and basis of accounting
- **Modified accrual basis** — Revenues recognized when measurable and available; expenditures recognized when the liability is incurred (with exceptions for long-term items)
- **Current financial resources** — The measurement focus of governmental funds, concerned with resources available to spend in the near term
- **Net position** — The government-wide equivalent of equity, classified as net investment in capital assets, restricted, or unrestricted
- **Encumbrance** — A commitment recorded when a purchase order is issued, reserving budgetary authority
