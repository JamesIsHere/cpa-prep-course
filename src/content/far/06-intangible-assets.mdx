# Intangible Assets

## Learning Objectives

- Distinguish between finite-life and indefinite-life intangible assets
- Account for the acquisition and amortization of intangible assets
- Apply goodwill impairment testing under ASC 350
- Identify the treatment of internally developed intangible assets and research and development costs

## Types of Intangible Assets

**Intangible assets** are identifiable nonmonetary assets without physical substance. They derive their value from contractual or legal rights, or from the ability to be separated from the entity and sold, transferred, or licensed. Common examples include:

- Patents
- Copyrights
- Trademarks and trade names
- Customer lists and relationships
- Franchise rights
- Noncompete agreements
- Internally developed software (post-feasibility)

Intangible assets are classified based on their useful life:

**Finite-life intangibles** have a determinable useful life. They are amortized over that life, typically using the straight-line method unless another pattern better reflects the economic benefits. Examples include patents (legal life of 20 years, but useful life may be shorter) and customer relationships.

**Indefinite-life intangibles** have no foreseeable limit on the period over which they are expected to generate cash flows. They are **not amortized** but are tested for impairment at least annually. Trademarks and certain broadcast licenses are common examples. The classification is reassessed each reporting period.

> Do not confuse "indefinite life" with "infinite life." An indefinite-life intangible has no predictable limit, but that assessment can change. If circumstances change and a useful life becomes determinable, the entity begins amortizing it.

## Goodwill

**Goodwill** is recognized only through a business combination. It represents the excess of the purchase price over the fair value of identifiable net assets acquired:

**Goodwill = Purchase price - Fair value of net identifiable assets**

Goodwill is never amortized under U.S. GAAP for public entities (private entities may elect to amortize it over 10 years or less under ASU 2014-02). It is assigned to **reporting units** and tested for impairment annually, or more frequently if triggering events occur.

### Goodwill Impairment Test (ASC 350, Simplified)

Under the current guidance, the impairment test is a **one-step quantitative approach**:

1. Compare the **fair value of the reporting unit** to its **carrying amount** (including goodwill)
2. If the carrying amount exceeds fair value, recognize an impairment loss equal to the difference, limited to the amount of goodwill allocated to that reporting unit

An entity may first perform an optional **qualitative assessment** (Step 0) to determine whether it is more likely than not that the fair value of a reporting unit is less than its carrying amount. If the entity concludes it is not more likely than not, no quantitative test is required.

### Worked Example

Company A acquires Company B for $5,000,000. The fair value of Company B's identifiable net assets is $4,200,000.

- Goodwill = $5,000,000 - $4,200,000 = **$800,000**

One year later, the reporting unit's carrying amount (including goodwill) is $4,800,000 and its fair value is $4,300,000.

- Impairment loss = $4,800,000 - $4,300,000 = **$500,000**
- Goodwill is written down from $800,000 to $300,000

> Goodwill impairment losses can never be reversed under U.S. GAAP. Once written down, the new carrying amount is the baseline going forward.

## Internally Developed Intangibles and R&D

Under U.S. GAAP, most **research and development (R&D)** costs are expensed as incurred. This includes salaries of R&D personnel, materials consumed, depreciation on R&D equipment with no alternative future use, and contract research services.

There is one major exception: **software development costs**. Under ASC 985 (software to be sold) and ASC 350-40 (internal-use software), costs incurred after **technological feasibility** is established (for external-use software) or after the **application development stage** begins (for internal-use software) are capitalized and amortized.

The stages for internal-use software are:

1. **Preliminary project stage** — Costs are expensed (conceptual planning, evaluating alternatives)
2. **Application development stage** — Costs are capitalized (coding, testing, installation)
3. **Post-implementation stage** — Costs are expensed (training, maintenance)

Other internally generated intangibles — such as internally developed brands, customer lists, and mastheads — are always expensed as incurred. They cannot be capitalized under U.S. GAAP.

## Impairment of Finite-Life Intangibles

Finite-life intangible assets follow the same impairment model as other long-lived assets under **ASC 360**:

1. **Recoverability test** — Compare the carrying amount to undiscounted future cash flows
2. **If impaired** — Write down to fair value; the loss equals carrying amount minus fair value

This is the same two-step approach used for PP&E. Impairment losses on finite-life intangibles held for use cannot be reversed.

## Key Terms

- **Goodwill** — The excess of the purchase price over the fair value of net identifiable assets acquired in a business combination
- **Indefinite-life intangible** — An intangible asset with no foreseeable limit on its cash-flow-generating period; not amortized, but tested for impairment annually
- **Amortization** — The systematic allocation of the cost of a finite-life intangible asset over its useful life
- **Reporting unit** — The level at which goodwill is tested for impairment, generally an operating segment or one level below
- **Technological feasibility** — The milestone after which software development costs may be capitalized under ASC 985

## Practice Problems

**1.** Which of the following intangible assets is NOT amortized?

a) A patent with a remaining legal life of 15 years
b) A customer relationship expected to generate cash flows for 8 years
c) A trademark with no foreseeable limit on its useful life
d) A franchise agreement with a 10-year term

**Answer:** c) A trademark with no foreseeable limit on its useful life — indefinite-life intangibles are not amortized but are tested for impairment at least annually.

---

**2.** A reporting unit has a carrying amount of $10 million, including $3 million of goodwill. The fair value of the reporting unit is $8.5 million. What impairment loss is recognized?

a) $0
b) $1,500,000
c) $3,000,000
d) $1,000,000

**Answer:** b) $1,500,000 — The carrying amount ($10M) exceeds fair value ($8.5M) by $1.5M. The impairment loss is $1.5M, which is less than the total goodwill of $3M, so it is fully recognized.

---

**3.** Under U.S. GAAP, costs incurred during the application development stage of an internal-use software project are:

a) Expensed as incurred
b) Capitalized and amortized
c) Capitalized but not amortized
d) Reported as research and development expense

**Answer:** b) Capitalized and amortized — costs in the application development stage are capitalized and then amortized over the software's estimated useful life once it is placed in service.
