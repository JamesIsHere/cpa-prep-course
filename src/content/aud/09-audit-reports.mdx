# Audit Reports

## Learning Objectives

- Identify the elements of a standard unmodified audit report
- Distinguish between the types of modified opinions and when each is appropriate
- Understand emphasis-of-matter and other-matter paragraphs
- Recognize reporting requirements for comparative financial statements and group audits

## The Standard Unmodified Opinion

The **unmodified opinion** (sometimes called a "clean" opinion) is issued when the auditor concludes that the financial statements are presented fairly, in all material respects, in accordance with the applicable financial reporting framework. It is the most common type of audit report.

### Required Elements

A standard unmodified audit report on financial statements of a nonpublic entity includes the following sections, in order:

1. **Title** — Must include the word "independent" (e.g., "Independent Auditor's Report")
2. **Addressee** — Typically addressed to the board of directors and shareholders
3. **Opinion section** — States the auditor's opinion first, identifies the financial statements audited, and names the entity and dates covered
4. **Basis for Opinion** — States the audit was conducted in accordance with GAAS, refers to auditor independence and ethical requirements, and asserts that sufficient appropriate evidence was obtained
5. **Responsibilities of Management** — Describes management's responsibility for the financial statements and internal controls
6. **Auditor's Responsibilities** — Describes what an audit involves, the concept of reasonable assurance, and the auditor's obligations
7. **Signature** — The firm's name (manual or printed)
8. **City and state** — Where the auditor practices
9. **Date** — The date of the auditor's report, which should be no earlier than the date the auditor obtained sufficient appropriate evidence

> The opinion section now appears first in the report, before the basis for opinion. This "opinion-first" format was adopted to make reports more useful to readers. Exam questions may test the correct order of report sections.

### Key Language

The opinion paragraph uses specific wording: the financial statements "present fairly, in all material respects" in accordance with the applicable framework. "Present fairly" is the standard for GAAS audits. PCAOB audits of public companies use similar language but follow a different report format.

## Modified Opinions

When the auditor cannot issue an unmodified opinion, one of three **modified opinions** is appropriate. The choice depends on two factors:

1. **The nature of the matter** — Are the financial statements materially misstated, or was the auditor unable to obtain sufficient evidence?
2. **The pervasiveness** — Is the effect material but limited, or material and pervasive?

| | Material but Not Pervasive | Material and Pervasive |
|---|---|---|
| **Financial statements are materially misstated** | Qualified opinion | Adverse opinion |
| **Unable to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence** | Qualified opinion | Disclaimer of opinion |

### Qualified Opinion

A **qualified opinion** states that "except for" the effects of a specific matter, the financial statements are presented fairly. It is used when:

- A misstatement is material but not pervasive, or
- The auditor was unable to obtain sufficient evidence about a matter that is material but not pervasive

The report includes a **Basis for Qualified Opinion** section that describes the matter giving rise to the qualification.

**Example scenario:** A company refuses to capitalize a lease that should be classified as a finance lease. The misstatement is material ($2 million impact on assets and liabilities) but is isolated to this one area and does not affect the rest of the financial statements. The auditor issues a qualified opinion.

### Adverse Opinion

An **adverse opinion** states that the financial statements are **not** presented fairly. It is issued when misstatements are both material and pervasive — meaning they affect multiple accounts, elements, or disclosures, or represent a substantial proportion of the financial statements.

The report includes a **Basis for Adverse Opinion** section explaining the misstatements and, when practicable, quantifying their effects.

**Example scenario:** A company fails to consolidate a significant subsidiary, distorting virtually every line item on the financial statements. The misstatement is material and pervasive, warranting an adverse opinion.

### Disclaimer of Opinion

A **disclaimer of opinion** states that the auditor does not express an opinion on the financial statements. It is issued when the auditor is unable to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence and the possible effects are both material and pervasive.

The report includes a **Basis for Disclaimer of Opinion** section. The Auditor's Responsibilities section is modified to remove language about obtaining reasonable assurance, since the auditor was unable to do so.

**Example scenario:** A company engaged the auditor after the physical inventory count was completed, and the auditor cannot satisfy themselves through alternative procedures. Inventory represents 70% of total assets. The limitation is material and pervasive, warranting a disclaimer.

> When deciding between qualified and adverse (or qualified and disclaimer), the key question is pervasiveness. If the matter is confined to a specific account or disclosure, it is likely "material but not pervasive" and a qualified opinion is appropriate. If it affects the financial statements broadly, it is "material and pervasive."

## Emphasis-of-Matter and Other-Matter Paragraphs

Sometimes the auditor wants to draw attention to information without modifying the opinion.

### Emphasis-of-Matter

An **emphasis-of-matter** paragraph highlights a matter that is appropriately presented or disclosed in the financial statements but is of such importance that it is fundamental to the user's understanding. Common uses include:

- Going concern uncertainty
- Significant related-party transactions
- A change in accounting principle
- A significant subsequent event
- Adoption of a new accounting standard

The paragraph appears after the opinion section, uses the heading "Emphasis of Matter," references where the matter is disclosed, and states that the opinion is not modified with respect to this matter.

### Other-Matter

An **other-matter** paragraph communicates information not presented or disclosed in the financial statements that is relevant to understanding the audit, the auditor's responsibilities, or the report. Examples include:

- The financial statements of the prior period were audited by a different auditor
- Restriction on distribution or use of the report
- The auditor's report on supplementary information

## Comparative Financial Statements

When comparative financial statements are presented, the auditor's report covers both periods. Several situations can arise:

**Different opinions for different periods** — The auditor can issue an unmodified opinion on the current year and a qualified opinion on the prior year within the same report.

**Updated opinion** — If circumstances that led to a prior modification have been resolved, the auditor can issue an updated (changed) opinion. The report should include an other-matter paragraph explaining the change.

**Prior-period audited by a predecessor** — The successor auditor can either reissue the predecessor's report or reference the predecessor's report in an other-matter paragraph.

## Going Concern

If the auditor concludes that **substantial doubt about the entity's ability to continue as a going concern** exists, the auditor should:

1. Evaluate management's plans to mitigate the going concern conditions
2. Determine whether adequate disclosure has been made
3. Include an emphasis-of-matter paragraph if the going concern uncertainty is adequately disclosed
4. Issue a qualified or adverse opinion if the disclosure is inadequate

The going concern evaluation covers a period of one year from the date the financial statements are issued (or available to be issued).

## Key Terms

- **Unmodified opinion** — An audit opinion stating that financial statements are presented fairly in all material respects
- **Qualified opinion** — An opinion stating that financial statements are fairly presented except for a specific material matter
- **Adverse opinion** — An opinion stating that financial statements are not presented fairly due to material and pervasive misstatements
- **Disclaimer of opinion** — A statement that the auditor does not express an opinion due to inability to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence
- **Emphasis-of-matter paragraph** — An additional paragraph drawing attention to a matter appropriately disclosed in the financial statements
- **Pervasive** — A term describing effects that are widespread across the financial statements, not confined to specific elements or accounts

## Practice Problems

**1.** A client refuses to disclose a material related-party transaction in the notes to the financial statements. The misstatement is material but confined to the disclosure and does not affect the recognition or measurement of other account balances. The auditor should issue:

a) An unmodified opinion with an emphasis-of-matter paragraph
b) A qualified opinion
c) An adverse opinion
d) A disclaimer of opinion

**Answer:** b) The omitted disclosure is a misstatement that is material but not pervasive (it is limited to one disclosure area). A qualified opinion with "except for" language is appropriate.

---

**2.** Which of the following situations would most likely result in a disclaimer of opinion?

a) The client changed its depreciation method without adequate disclosure
b) The auditor was unable to observe inventory or perform alternative procedures, and inventory is 60% of total assets
c) The client failed to capitalize a material lease
d) The auditor identified a material uncertainty about going concern

**Answer:** b) A disclaimer is issued when the auditor cannot obtain sufficient appropriate evidence and the possible effects are material and pervasive. Inventory representing 60% of assets would be pervasive.

---

**3.** An auditor issues an unmodified opinion but wants to draw attention to the entity's adoption of a new revenue recognition standard. This information should be communicated in:

a) The Basis for Opinion section
b) An emphasis-of-matter paragraph
c) An other-matter paragraph
d) A qualified opinion

**Answer:** b) A change in accounting principle that is appropriately applied and disclosed is communicated through an emphasis-of-matter paragraph. The opinion is not modified.
