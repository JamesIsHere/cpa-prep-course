# Review and Compilation Engagements

## Learning Objectives

- Distinguish between review and compilation engagements in terms of assurance provided
- Understand the procedures required for a review engagement under SSARS
- Identify the accountant's responsibilities in a compilation engagement
- Recognize the reporting requirements for reviews and compilations

## The Assurance Spectrum

Not every client needs a full audit. **Statements on Standards for Accounting and Review Services (SSARS)** govern two types of engagements that provide less than the reasonable assurance of an audit:

| Engagement | Assurance Level | Key Procedures | Report Expression |
|------------|----------------|----------------|-------------------|
| Audit | Reasonable (high) assurance | Risk assessment, tests of controls, substantive procedures | Opinion |
| Review | Limited assurance | Inquiry and analytical procedures | Conclusion |
| Compilation | No assurance | Reading the financial statements | None |

These engagements apply to financial statements of **nonpublic entities** (non-issuers). Public companies are subject to PCAOB standards and do not have compilation or SSARS review options.

## Review Engagements

A **review engagement** provides **limited assurance** that no material modifications should be made to the financial statements for them to be in accordance with the applicable financial reporting framework. The accountant expresses this as **negative assurance** — stating that nothing came to the accountant's attention that would require material modification.

### Procedures

Review procedures are substantially less in scope than an audit. The two primary procedures are:

**Inquiry** — The accountant asks management and relevant personnel about:
- The entity's accounting principles and practices
- Unusual or complex transactions
- Subsequent events
- Fraud or suspected fraud
- Known litigation and claims
- Actions taken at board or shareholder meetings

**Analytical procedures** — The accountant evaluates financial data by analyzing plausible relationships:
- Compare current-period amounts to prior periods
- Compare recorded amounts to expected amounts developed by the accountant
- Compare financial information to industry data
- Evaluate relationships among financial statement elements that would be expected to follow a predictable pattern

The accountant should also:
- Obtain knowledge of the entity's industry and business
- Read the financial statements and consider whether they appear to be appropriate in form
- Obtain a **representation letter** from management

### What a Review Does NOT Include

A review does not include:
- Obtaining an understanding of internal controls
- Testing controls
- Inspecting documents or confirming balances with third parties
- Performing tests of details
- Physical inspection of assets

> If during a review the accountant becomes aware of information that may be materially incorrect, the accountant should perform additional procedures to determine whether a material modification is needed. This does not convert the review into an audit — it simply means the accountant must resolve the question.

### The Review Report

The review report includes:

1. **Title** — Must include the word "independent"
2. **Addressee** — Typically the entity's management or board
3. **Conclusion** — The accountant's limited assurance conclusion (negative assurance)
4. **Basis for conclusion** — States the review was conducted in accordance with SSARS
5. **Management's responsibility** — For preparation and fair presentation
6. **Accountant's responsibility** — To conduct the review in accordance with SSARS
7. **Signature, city, and date**

The conclusion paragraph states: "Based on my (our) review, I am (we are) not aware of any material modifications that should be made to the accompanying financial statements in order for them to be in accordance with [applicable framework]."

## Compilation Engagements

A **compilation engagement** provides **no assurance** on the financial statements. The accountant assists management in presenting financial information in the form of financial statements without undertaking to provide any assurance.

### Requirements

The accountant should:
- Obtain an understanding of the entity's industry and business
- Understand the applicable financial reporting framework
- Read the compiled financial statements and consider whether they are free from obvious material errors
- Possess the appropriate competence to perform the compilation

The accountant is **not required** to:
- Make inquiries of management
- Perform analytical procedures
- Verify, corroborate, or review any information provided by management
- Obtain a representation letter

### Independence and Compilations

A significant distinction from audits and reviews: the accountant **is not required to be independent** to perform a compilation. However, if the accountant is not independent, this must be disclosed in the compilation report. The accountant may (but is not required to) disclose the reason for the lack of independence.

### Omission of Disclosures

Management may elect to omit substantially all disclosures required by the applicable framework. This is acceptable in a compilation as long as:
- The omission is clearly indicated in the report
- The omission is not, to the accountant's knowledge, undertaken to mislead users
- The report includes a specific paragraph noting the omission

This option does not exist for audits or reviews.

### The Compilation Report

The compilation report includes:

1. **Title** — Does not need to include "independent" (since independence is not required)
2. **Addressee** — Typically the entity's management
3. **Statement that management is responsible** for the financial statements
4. **Statement that the accountant performed the compilation** in accordance with SSARS
5. **Statement that the accountant has not audited or reviewed** the financial statements and does not express an opinion or conclusion
6. **Signature, city, and date**

## Engagement Letters

Both review and compilation engagements require an **engagement letter** that establishes the terms, including:
- The objective and scope of the engagement
- Management's responsibilities
- The accountant's responsibilities
- The limitations of the engagement
- The expected form of the report

The engagement letter should be signed by both the accountant and management before the engagement begins.

## Changing Engagements

Sometimes a client requests a change from a higher level of service to a lower one — for example, from an audit to a review, or from a review to a compilation. The accountant should consider:

- **The reason for the change** — Is it legitimate (change in circumstances, revised requirements) or an attempt to limit the scope of the engagement?
- **The additional effort required** to complete the original engagement
- **The cost implications** of additional procedures

**Acceptable reasons** for a downgrade include a change in requirements by a third party (e.g., a bank no longer requires an audit), a change in the client's needs, or a misunderstanding about the nature of the engagement.

**Unacceptable reasons** include attempting to prevent the accountant from discovering a misstatement or restricting access to information.

If the accountant agrees to the change, the report should refer only to the lower-level engagement. No reference should be made to the original, higher-level engagement or to any procedures already performed.

> If the client requests a downgrade during an audit and the auditor has already discovered potential misstatements, this is a red flag. The auditor should carefully evaluate whether the request is an attempt to conceal problems.

## Key Terms

- **Limited assurance** — A level of assurance lower than reasonable assurance, obtained through inquiry and analytical procedures in a review engagement
- **Negative assurance** — A conclusion stating that nothing came to the accountant's attention requiring material modification
- **SSARS** — Statements on Standards for Accounting and Review Services, governing reviews and compilations of nonpublic entity financial statements
- **Compilation** — An engagement where the accountant assists in presenting financial information in financial statement form without providing any assurance
- **Representation letter** — A written confirmation from management required in review engagements (but not compilations)

## Practice Problems

**1.** In a review engagement, the accountant's report provides:

a) Reasonable assurance that the financial statements are free of material misstatement
b) Limited assurance expressed as negative assurance
c) No assurance on the financial statements
d) Absolute assurance that the financial statements are accurate

**Answer:** b) A review engagement provides limited assurance. The accountant's conclusion is expressed as negative assurance — that nothing came to the accountant's attention requiring material modification to the financial statements.

---

**2.** Which of the following is an accountant required to do in a compilation engagement?

a) Perform analytical procedures on the financial data
b) Obtain a management representation letter
c) Verify the information supplied by management
d) Read the compiled financial statements for obvious material errors

**Answer:** d) In a compilation, the accountant reads the financial statements and considers whether they are free from obvious material errors. The accountant is not required to perform analytical procedures, verify information, or obtain a representation letter.

---

**3.** A CPA is performing a compilation and is not independent with respect to the client. The CPA should:

a) Decline the engagement
b) Disclose the lack of independence in the compilation report
c) Convert the engagement to a review
d) Obtain a waiver of independence from the AICPA

**Answer:** b) Independence is not required for compilations. However, if the accountant is not independent, this fact must be disclosed in the compilation report. The accountant may optionally disclose the reason for the lack of independence.
