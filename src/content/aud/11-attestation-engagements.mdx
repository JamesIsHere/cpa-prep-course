# Attestation Engagements

## Learning Objectives

- Understand the scope of attestation engagements under SSAE
- Distinguish between examination, review, and agreed-upon procedures engagements
- Identify the reporting requirements for each type of attestation engagement
- Apply attestation standards to common subject matters tested on the CPA exam

## What Are Attestation Engagements?

**Attestation engagements** are a broad category of services where a CPA evaluates subject matter (or an assertion about subject matter) against suitable criteria and issues a report. They are governed by **Statements on Standards for Attestation Engagements (SSAE)**.

The key distinction from audit and review engagements under SAS/SSARS is the **subject matter**. While audits and reviews address historical financial statements, attestation engagements can address a wide range of subject matter, including:

- Prospective financial information (forecasts and projections)
- Internal controls over financial reporting (for non-issuers)
- Compliance with laws, regulations, or contractual requirements
- Pro forma financial information
- Management's Discussion and Analysis (MD&A)
- Service organization controls (SOC reports)

### Preconditions for an Attestation Engagement

Before accepting an attestation engagement, the practitioner must confirm:

1. The subject matter is appropriate (it can be evaluated against suitable criteria)
2. The **criteria** used are suitable and available to users
3. The practitioner has the necessary competence
4. The practitioner has access to the evidence needed
5. The practitioner's conclusion or findings will be included in a written report

**Suitable criteria** are objective, measurable, complete, and relevant. They must be available to the intended users — either through a publicly available framework, inclusion in the report, or other means.

## Three Types of Attestation Engagements

### Examination Engagement

An **examination engagement** provides the highest level of attestation assurance. The practitioner obtains **reasonable assurance** and expresses an **opinion** on the subject matter.

The procedures in an examination are similar in rigor to an audit:
- Obtain an understanding of the subject matter
- Assess the risks of material misstatement
- Design and perform procedures responsive to those risks
- Evaluate the evidence obtained and form an opinion

**Examination report:**
- Expresses an opinion on whether the subject matter (or assertion) is presented in accordance with the criteria, in all material respects
- Uses the same modification framework as audit opinions (unmodified, qualified, adverse, disclaimer)

**Example:** A practitioner is engaged to examine a company's assertion that its internal controls over financial reporting are effective as of December 31. After performing risk assessment, testing controls, and evaluating results, the practitioner issues an opinion on the effectiveness of internal controls.

### Review Engagement (Attestation)

An **attestation review engagement** provides **limited assurance** on the subject matter. The procedures are substantially less than those in an examination.

Procedures include:
- Obtaining an understanding of the subject matter
- Making inquiries
- Performing analytical procedures and other procedures the practitioner considers appropriate

**Attestation review report:**
- Expresses negative assurance — the practitioner states that nothing came to their attention indicating the subject matter is not in accordance with the criteria

**Example:** A practitioner is engaged to review a company's compliance with specific environmental regulations. After inquiry and analysis, the practitioner reports whether anything came to their attention suggesting non-compliance.

> Do not confuse an attestation review with a financial statement review under SSARS. Both provide limited assurance, but they are governed by different standards and apply to different subject matter.

### Agreed-Upon Procedures Engagement

An **agreed-upon procedures (AUP)** engagement is fundamentally different from examination and review engagements. The practitioner performs only the specific procedures that the engaging party agrees to and reports the **findings** without providing an opinion or conclusion.

Key characteristics:
- The procedures are developed by the engaging party (and potentially the responsible party or other specified parties)
- The practitioner makes no judgment about the sufficiency of the procedures
- The report presents factual findings, not assurance
- Users draw their own conclusions from the findings

**AUP report includes:**
- A description of the procedures performed
- The findings for each procedure
- A statement that the practitioner was not engaged to perform an examination or review and does not express an opinion or conclusion
- A statement that the report is intended solely for the use of the specified parties

**Example:** A franchisor engages a practitioner to perform specific procedures on a franchisee's sales reports — such as recalculating royalty computations, tracing sales to source documents, and comparing reported sales to bank deposits. The practitioner reports the results of each procedure but does not provide any overall assurance.

> Under the current SSAE standards, agreed-upon procedures reports are no longer restricted to specified parties. The report can be used by anyone, but it still describes the procedures and findings without providing assurance.

## Common Attestation Subject Matters

### Prospective Financial Information

**Forecasts** are prospective financial statements that present expected financial position, results of operations, and cash flows based on expected conditions and expected courses of action.

**Projections** are prospective financial statements that present financial results based on one or more hypothetical assumptions ("what-if" scenarios).

| | Forecast | Projection |
|---|---|---|
| Basis | Expected conditions | Hypothetical assumptions |
| Engagement types | Examination only | Examination only |
| Distribution | General use or limited use | Limited use only |

A practitioner cannot perform a review or compilation of a forecast or projection. Only examination is allowed.

> Projections are restricted to limited use because they are based on hypothetical assumptions that may not materialize. General distribution could mislead users who do not understand the assumptions.

### Service Organization Controls (SOC Reports)

**SOC reports** are attestation engagements that evaluate the controls at a service organization (such as a payroll processor, cloud hosting provider, or data center).

- **SOC 1** — Controls relevant to user entities' financial reporting (uses the SSAE framework). Directly relevant to audits of user entities.
- **SOC 2** — Controls relevant to security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, and privacy (Trust Services Criteria).
- **SOC 3** — A general-use report based on the same criteria as SOC 2, but in a shorter format suitable for public distribution.

**Type 1 reports** cover the design and implementation of controls at a specific point in time.
**Type 2 reports** cover the design, implementation, and operating effectiveness of controls over a period of time.

### Compliance Attestation

A practitioner may be engaged to examine or perform agreed-upon procedures on an entity's compliance with specified requirements (regulatory, contractual, or grant-related). The criteria are the specific requirements themselves — the practitioner evaluates whether the entity has complied.

## AT-C Standards Framework

The attestation standards are organized under **AT-C sections** (clarified attestation standards). Understanding the numbering helps navigate exam questions:

| AT-C Section | Topic | Engagement Type |
|-------------|-------|-----------------|
| AT-C 105 | Concepts Common to All Attestation Engagements | General |
| AT-C 205 | Examination Engagements | Examination |
| AT-C 210 | Review Engagements | Review |
| AT-C 215 | Agreed-Upon Procedures Engagements | AUP |
| AT-C 305 | Prospective Financial Information | Examination |
| AT-C 320 | Reporting on an Examination of Controls at a Service Organization (SOC) | Examination |
| AT-C 395 | Management's Discussion and Analysis | Examination or Review |

### AT-C vs. AU-C vs. AR-C

- **AU-C** — Auditing standards (SAS) for audits of historical financial statements
- **AR-C** — Accounting and review standards (SSARS) for compilations and reviews of non-issuers
- **AT-C** — Attestation standards (SSAE) for examination, review, and AUP on subject matter other than historical financial statements

> The exam may test which standard applies to a given engagement. The key distinction is the subject matter: historical financial statements fall under AU-C (audit) or AR-C (review/compilation), while all other subject matter falls under AT-C (attestation).

## Compliance Attestation — Depth

Compliance attestation engagements evaluate whether an entity has complied with specific requirements. These requirements can come from laws, regulations, contracts, or grant agreements.

### Types of Compliance Engagements

**Examination of compliance** — The practitioner obtains reasonable assurance and expresses an opinion on whether the entity complied with the specified requirements in all material respects. This is similar in rigor to a financial statement audit.

**Agreed-upon procedures on compliance** — The practitioner performs only the specific procedures agreed upon by the engaging parties and reports findings without providing assurance. Common in government grant compliance.

### Government Compliance Requirements

Many compliance attestation engagements arise from government requirements:

- **Single Audit Act** — Entities spending $750,000 or more in federal awards must have a single audit (or program-specific audit). The auditor tests compliance with requirements applicable to major federal programs.
- **Yellow Book (Government Auditing Standards)** — Published by the GAO, these standards apply to audits of government entities and entities receiving government funding. They impose additional requirements beyond GAAS, including independence standards and reporting on internal control and compliance.

### SOC Engagement Types — Detail

| Report | Subject Matter | Criteria | Primary Users |
|--------|---------------|----------|---------------|
| SOC 1 Type 1 | Controls relevant to user financial reporting | SSAE (AT-C 320) | User auditors, management |
| SOC 1 Type 2 | Same, tested over a period | SSAE (AT-C 320) | User auditors, management |
| SOC 2 Type 1 | Controls at a service org — security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, privacy | Trust Services Criteria | Management, regulators, specified parties |
| SOC 2 Type 2 | Same, tested over a period | Trust Services Criteria | Management, regulators, specified parties |
| SOC 3 | Same as SOC 2 | Trust Services Criteria | General use (public) |
| SOC for Cybersecurity | Entity-wide cybersecurity risk management program | AICPA cybersecurity criteria | General use (public) |
| SOC for Supply Chain | Supply chain risk management controls | Trust Services Criteria | Management, business partners |

> SOC 1 is the only SOC report directly relevant to the financial statement audit (it helps the user auditor evaluate controls at the service organization). SOC 2 and SOC 3 address broader operational controls. The exam frequently tests the distinction between SOC 1, 2, and 3.

## Key Terms

- **SSAE** — Statements on Standards for Attestation Engagements, the standards governing examination, review, and agreed-upon procedures engagements on subject matter other than historical financial statements
- **Examination engagement** — An attestation engagement providing reasonable assurance, resulting in an opinion
- **Agreed-upon procedures** — An engagement where the practitioner performs only specific procedures agreed to by the engaging party and reports findings without assurance
- **Forecast** — Prospective financial statements based on expected conditions and expected courses of action
- **Projection** — Prospective financial statements based on hypothetical assumptions
- **SOC report** — An attestation report on the controls at a service organization

## Practice Problems

**1.** A practitioner is engaged to perform an agreed-upon procedures engagement on a company's royalty payments. The practitioner's report should include:

a) An opinion on whether the royalty payments are fairly stated
b) Limited assurance that no material misstatements exist in the royalty computations
c) A description of the procedures performed and the findings
d) A recommendation for improvements to the royalty calculation process

**Answer:** c) In an agreed-upon procedures engagement, the practitioner reports the procedures performed and the factual findings. No opinion, conclusion, or assurance is expressed. Users draw their own conclusions.

---

**2.** Which type of prospective financial information may be distributed for general use?

a) Both forecasts and projections
b) Forecasts only
c) Projections only
d) Neither forecasts nor projections

**Answer:** b) Forecasts are based on expected conditions and may be distributed for general use. Projections are based on hypothetical assumptions and are restricted to limited use — parties who are in a position to understand the hypothetical assumptions.

---

**3.** A SOC 1 Type 2 report provides information about:

a) The design of controls at a service organization at a point in time
b) The security and availability controls at a service organization
c) The design, implementation, and operating effectiveness of controls relevant to user entities' financial reporting over a period of time
d) A general-use summary of the service organization's security practices

**Answer:** c) A SOC 1 report addresses controls relevant to financial reporting, and a Type 2 report covers design, implementation, and operating effectiveness over a period of time (not just at a point in time).
