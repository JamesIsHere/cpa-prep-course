# Quality Management

## Learning Objectives

- Understand the firm-level quality management standards (SQMS No. 1 and No. 2)
- Describe the components of a system of quality management
- Explain engagement-level quality management requirements
- Identify the role of peer review in the profession

## Why Quality Management Matters

An audit opinion is only as credible as the firm that issues it. **Quality management** encompasses the policies, procedures, and culture that a firm establishes to ensure its engagements are performed consistently and in accordance with professional standards.

In 2022, the AICPA adopted new quality management standards that replaced the older quality control (QC) standards. The shift from "quality control" to "quality management" reflects a more proactive, risk-based approach — firms must now identify risks to quality and design responses, rather than simply implementing a static set of policies.

The three quality management standards are:

- **SQMS No. 1** — A Firm's System of Quality Management
- **SQMS No. 2** — Engagement Quality Reviews
- **SAS No. 146 (QMS)** — Quality Management for an Engagement Conducted in Accordance with GAAS

## Firm-Level Quality Management: SQMS No. 1

**SQMS No. 1** requires every firm that performs engagements under the SAS, SSARS, or SSAE standards to design, implement, and operate a system of quality management. The firm's leadership bears ultimate responsibility for this system.

### The Eight Components

The system of quality management consists of eight interrelated components:

**1. The Firm's Risk Assessment Process**

The firm must establish a process to identify and assess **quality risks** — conditions, events, or circumstances that could adversely affect the quality of engagements. The firm then designs and implements responses to those risks.

This mirrors the audit risk model, but applied to the firm itself. The firm identifies what could go wrong with engagement quality and takes steps to prevent it.

**2. Governance and Leadership**

Firm leadership must establish the firm's culture and demonstrate a commitment to quality through:
- Tone at the top emphasizing quality over commercial considerations
- Organizational structure that supports quality
- Assignment of roles and responsibilities for quality management
- Financial and operational decisions that do not compromise quality

**3. Relevant Ethical Requirements**

The firm must establish policies to ensure compliance with ethical requirements, most importantly **independence**. This includes:
- Identifying and evaluating threats to independence
- Monitoring compliance with independence requirements
- Confirming independence at least annually for each engagement
- Addressing breaches when they occur

**4. Acceptance and Continuance of Client Relationships and Engagements**

The firm's acceptance and continuance process should evaluate:
- Management's integrity
- Whether the firm has the competence, resources, and time to perform the engagement
- Whether the firm can comply with ethical requirements (particularly independence)
- Whether significant issues from prior engagements have been resolved

**5. Engagement Performance**

This component addresses how engagements are actually carried out, including:
- Direction, supervision, and review of engagement work
- Consultation on difficult or contentious matters
- Resolution of differences of opinion within the engagement team
- Assembly and retention of engagement documentation

**6. Resources**

The firm must ensure it has sufficient and appropriate resources, including:
- **Human resources** — Personnel with the right competence and capabilities, proper recruitment, training, and evaluation
- **Technological resources** — Audit software, data analytics tools, IT infrastructure
- **Intellectual resources** — Methodologies, templates, industry guidance, knowledge-sharing systems

**7. Information and Communication**

The firm must establish channels for communicating quality-related information both internally and externally. This includes reporting on the quality management system, communicating with external parties (regulators, peer reviewers), and ensuring engagement teams have access to relevant information.

**8. The Monitoring and Remediation Process**

The firm must actively monitor its system of quality management and take corrective action when deficiencies are identified. This includes:
- Ongoing monitoring activities (inspection of completed engagements)
- Periodic evaluations of the system as a whole
- Root-cause analysis of identified deficiencies
- Remediation of deficiencies
- An annual evaluation and conclusion on whether the system provides reasonable assurance of quality

> The monitoring and remediation process requires the firm to reach an annual conclusion about whether the quality management system is operating effectively. This is a significant accountability requirement that the exam tests.

## Engagement Quality Reviews: SQMS No. 2

An **engagement quality review (EQR)** is an independent evaluation of significant judgments made by the engagement team and the conclusions reached. The engagement quality reviewer is a partner or other qualified person who is **not** a member of the engagement team.

### When Is an EQR Required?

The firm's policies determine which engagements require an EQR. At minimum, standards require EQRs for:
- Audits of financial statements of entities with public interest characteristics (such as SEC registrants)
- Engagements where law or regulation requires it
- Other engagements where the firm determines an EQR is an appropriate response to assessed quality risks

### The Reviewer's Responsibilities

The engagement quality reviewer evaluates:
- Significant judgments made by the engagement team, including judgments related to independence, risk assessment, and materiality
- The appropriateness of the audit opinion or conclusion
- Whether consultation was sought on difficult matters and whether conclusions from those consultations were implemented
- Whether the engagement documentation supports the conclusions

The EQR must be completed before the engagement report is issued. The reviewer must be satisfied that all significant matters have been resolved.

## Engagement-Level Quality Management

**SAS No. 146** addresses quality management at the individual engagement level. The engagement partner has overall responsibility for quality on each engagement, including:

- Ensuring the engagement team collectively has the competence and capabilities to perform the engagement
- Directing, supervising, and reviewing the work
- Taking responsibility for appropriate consultation
- Taking responsibility for resolving differences of opinion
- Taking responsibility for the engagement documentation

### Documentation

Audit documentation (**working papers**) must be sufficient to enable an experienced auditor with no prior connection to the engagement to understand:
- The nature, timing, and extent of procedures performed
- The evidence obtained
- The conclusions reached

Documentation must be assembled in the final engagement file within **60 days** of the report release date. After that date, documents should not be deleted or discarded, though additions may be made if properly documented.

> The 60-day assembly period is a common exam question. Remember: documentation assembly must be complete within 60 days of the report release date, and the documentation retention period is a minimum of five years from the report release date.

## Peer Review

**Peer review** is the profession's external quality monitoring mechanism. AICPA member firms that perform audits, reviews, compilations, or other attest services are required to undergo a peer review every three years.

### Types of Peer Review

**System review** — For firms that perform audits or examinations. The peer reviewer evaluates the firm's system of quality management and selects engagements for review. The reviewer issues a report with one of three ratings:

- **Pass** — The system is suitably designed and operating effectively
- **Pass with deficiencies** — The system is suitably designed and operating effectively, except for certain deficiencies
- **Fail** — The system is not suitably designed or not operating effectively

**Engagement review** — For firms that only perform reviews, compilations, or preparation engagements (no audits). The peer reviewer selects engagements and evaluates whether they were performed in accordance with applicable standards. The same three ratings apply.

### Consequences

Peer review results can lead to:
- Corrective actions required by the administering entity
- Accelerated (shortened) review cycle
- In severe cases, referral to the AICPA Professional Ethics Division or state boards
- Impact on the firm's ability to maintain AICPA membership and practice permits

## PCAOB vs. AICPA Standards Comparison

For the CPA exam, it is important to understand the key differences between **PCAOB standards** (governing audits of public companies/issuers) and **AICPA GAAS** (governing audits of non-issuers/private companies).

### Structural Differences

| Feature | PCAOB (Issuers) | AICPA GAAS (Non-Issuers) |
|---------|-----------------|--------------------------|
| Standard setter | PCAOB (overseen by SEC) | AICPA Auditing Standards Board (ASB) |
| Quality control | PCAOB inspection program | Peer review (every 3 years) |
| Internal control reporting | Required integrated audit (opinion on ICFR) | Not required (but must understand controls) |
| Partner rotation | Required by SOX (5-year rotation) | Recommended but not required for most |
| CAMs/KAMs | Required (Critical Audit Matters) | Not required |
| Audit committee | Required for issuers | Not required (depends on entity) |
| Auditor tenure disclosure | Required in the report | Not required |

### PCAOB Inspection Program

The PCAOB conducts **inspections** of registered public accounting firms:

- Firms auditing more than 100 issuers are inspected **annually**
- Firms auditing 100 or fewer issuers are inspected at least **every three years** (triennially)
- Inspections evaluate both the firm's quality control system and selected engagements
- Findings can result in sanctions, censures, or restrictions on the firm's ability to audit public companies

### Integrated Audit Requirements

For public company audits, the PCAOB requires an **integrated audit** — the auditor simultaneously:

1. Audits the financial statements (expresses an opinion on fair presentation)
2. Audits internal control over financial reporting (expresses an opinion on effectiveness of ICFR)

Both opinions are included in a single report. The auditor must identify any **material weaknesses** in ICFR, which automatically result in an adverse opinion on internal controls.

> The integrated audit concept is unique to PCAOB/public company audits. Non-issuer audits under GAAS do not require an opinion on internal controls, though the auditor must still obtain an understanding of controls as part of risk assessment.

## Key Terms

- **SQMS No. 1** — The standard requiring firms to design, implement, and operate a system of quality management
- **Engagement quality review** — An independent evaluation of significant judgments and conclusions on an engagement, performed by a qualified reviewer not on the engagement team
- **Quality risk** — A condition, event, or circumstance that could adversely affect the quality of an engagement
- **Peer review** — A periodic external review of a firm's quality management system and engagement quality, required every three years for AICPA member firms
- **Monitoring and remediation** — The ongoing process of evaluating whether the quality management system is operating effectively and correcting deficiencies
