# Owner-Entity Transactions

## Learning Objectives

- Distinguish debt from equity in shareholder loans and apply below-market loan rules
- Identify constructive dividend situations and their tax consequences
- Apply Section 267 related party and Section 318 constructive ownership rules
- Analyze stock redemptions under Section 302 and partnership distributions

## Shareholder Loans: Debt vs. Equity

When a shareholder lends money to a corporation, the IRS may recharacterize the loan as an **equity contribution** if the transaction lacks the substance of true debt. The distinction matters because interest on debt is deductible by the corporation, while dividends on equity are not.

### Factors Courts Consider

| Factor | Favors Debt | Favors Equity |
|--------|------------|---------------|
| Fixed maturity date | Yes — definite repayment schedule | No maturity or indefinite term |
| Unconditional obligation to repay | Enforceable promissory note | Repayment contingent on earnings |
| Debt-to-equity ratio | Reasonable leverage | Thin capitalization (excessive debt relative to equity) |
| Proportionality to stock ownership | Loans not proportional to ownership | Loans mirror stock percentages |
| Subordination | Not subordinated to other creditors | Subordinated to all other debt |
| Source of payments | Fixed interest regardless of earnings | Payments only when profits exist |

> **Exam tip:** No single factor is determinative. The exam tests whether a set of facts, taken as a whole, indicates debt or equity. Thin capitalization (extremely high debt-to-equity ratio) is one of the strongest indicators of equity disguised as debt.

## Below-Market Loans — Section 7872

When a loan between related parties carries an interest rate below the **Applicable Federal Rate (AFR)**, Section 7872 requires the lender to recognize **imputed interest** income.

### Types of Below-Market Loans

| Loan Type | Treatment |
|-----------|-----------|
| Gift loans (family) | Foregone interest treated as gift from lender to borrower, then interest income from borrower to lender |
| Compensation-related loans (employer to employee) | Foregone interest treated as compensation to borrower, then interest income to lender |
| Corporation-shareholder loans | Foregone interest treated as dividend to shareholder, then interest payment from shareholder to corporation |

### $10,000 and $100,000 De Minimis Exceptions

- Loans of **$10,000 or less** are exempt from Section 7872 (unless tax avoidance is a principal purpose)
- For gift loans between **$10,001 and $100,000**, imputed interest is limited to the borrower's net investment income. If net investment income is $1,000 or less, it is treated as zero

**Worked Example:**

A corporation loans its sole shareholder $500,000 at 0% interest. The AFR is 5%.

| Step | Calculation | Amount |
|------|------------|--------|
| Foregone interest | $500,000 x 5% | $25,000 |
| Treated as dividend to shareholder | | $25,000 |
| Treated as interest income to corporation | | $25,000 |

The shareholder has $25,000 of dividend income and $25,000 of potential interest deduction (subject to investment interest limitations). The corporation has $25,000 of interest income and a corresponding $25,000 dividend paid (not deductible).

## Constructive Dividends

A **constructive dividend** occurs when a corporation confers an economic benefit on a shareholder that is not formally declared as a dividend. The IRS recharacterizes the transaction as a dividend to the extent of the corporation's E&P.

### Common Constructive Dividend Situations

| Situation | Tax Consequence |
|-----------|----------------|
| Excessive compensation | Amount exceeding reasonable compensation is a constructive dividend to the shareholder-employee |
| Personal use of corporate property | FMV of personal use (auto, vacation home, aircraft) is dividend income |
| Bargain sales to shareholders | Difference between FMV and sale price is dividend income |
| Rent-free use of corporate property | FMV of rent is dividend income |
| Shareholder expenses paid by corporation | Personal expenses paid by corporation are dividends |
| Loans with no intent to repay | Reclassified as dividend distributions |

**Worked Example:**

A C corporation pays its sole shareholder-officer $800,000 in salary. Comparable officers in similar companies earn $500,000.

| Item | Amount | Treatment |
|------|--------|-----------|
| Reasonable compensation | $500,000 | Deductible salary expense |
| Excess compensation | $300,000 | Constructive dividend (not deductible by corporation) |
| Tax to shareholder | $300,000 | Taxed as qualified dividend income |

> **Exam tip:** Constructive dividends create a double hit — the corporation loses the deduction for the excess amount, and the shareholder must report dividend income. This makes the combined tax cost substantially higher than simply paying a deductible salary.

## Section 267 — Related Party Rules

### Loss Disallowance

No deduction is allowed for losses on sales or exchanges of property between **related parties** under Section 267. Related parties include:

- Family members (siblings, spouse, ancestors, lineal descendants — NOT in-laws)
- An individual and a corporation in which the individual owns more than 50% (directly or constructively)
- Two corporations that are members of the same controlled group
- A corporation and a partnership if the same persons own more than 50% of each

The disallowed loss is not permanently lost. The **related buyer** may reduce any future gain on resale by the previously disallowed loss, but cannot create or increase a loss.

### Matching Rule for Accrued Expenses

When a related accrual-basis payor accrues an expense owed to a related cash-basis payee, the deduction is deferred until the payee **includes the amount in income** (generally when paid). This prevents mismatched timing.

**Worked Example:**

Father sells stock to Son for $30,000. Father's basis is $50,000.

| Step | Calculation | Amount |
|------|------------|--------|
| Realized loss | $30,000 - $50,000 | ($20,000) |
| **Recognized loss** | Disallowed under Section 267 | **$0** |

Son later sells the stock to an unrelated party for $55,000. Son's basis is $30,000 (purchase price).

| Step | Calculation | Amount |
|------|------------|--------|
| Realized gain | $55,000 - $30,000 | $25,000 |
| Previously disallowed loss | | ($20,000) |
| **Recognized gain** | $25,000 - $20,000 | **$5,000** |

## Section 318 — Constructive Ownership

Section 318 attributes stock ownership among related parties for purposes of determining control, related party status, and redemption treatment.

### Attribution Rules

| Source | Attribution |
|--------|-----------|
| Family | Stock owned by spouse, children, grandchildren, and parents is attributed to the taxpayer (NOT siblings) |
| Entity to owner | Stock owned by a partnership, estate, trust, or corporation (50%+ shareholder) is attributed proportionally to the partners, beneficiaries, or shareholders |
| Owner to entity | Stock owned by a partner or 50%+ shareholder is attributed to the partnership or corporation |
| Options | A person holding an option to acquire stock is treated as owning that stock |

> **Exam tip:** Family attribution under Section 318 does NOT include siblings. This is a frequent exam trap. Also, stock attributed from an entity to an owner can be re-attributed, but stock attributed from one family member cannot be re-attributed to another family member (no "double family" attribution).

## Stock Redemptions — Section 302

A **redemption** is a corporation's acquisition of its own stock from a shareholder in exchange for property (usually cash). The key question is whether the redemption is treated as a **sale/exchange** (capital gain/loss) or as a **distribution** (dividend to the extent of E&P).

### Section 302(b) Tests — Sale/Exchange Treatment

| Test | Requirement |
|------|------------|
| 302(b)(1) — Not essentially equivalent to a dividend | The redemption results in a "meaningful reduction" in the shareholder's proportionate interest (facts and circumstances) |
| 302(b)(2) — Substantially disproportionate | After the redemption, the shareholder owns less than 50% of total voting power AND less than 80% of the percentage owned before the redemption |
| 302(b)(3) — Complete termination | The shareholder's entire interest in the corporation is redeemed; family attribution may be waived if conditions are met |
| 302(b)(4) — Partial liquidation | Distribution is not essentially equivalent to a dividend at the corporate level (corporate contraction) |

**Worked Example:**

A shareholder owns 60 out of 100 outstanding shares (60%). The corporation redeems 25 shares.

| Metric | Before | After | Test |
|--------|--------|-------|------|
| Shares owned | 60 | 35 | |
| Total outstanding | 100 | 75 | |
| Ownership percentage | 60% | 46.67% | Below 50% |
| 80% of prior percentage | | 48% (80% x 60%) | 46.67% < 48% |
| **302(b)(2) met?** | | | **Yes** |

The redemption qualifies as substantially disproportionate, so it receives sale/exchange treatment.

## Partnership Distributions

### Current Distributions (Non-Liquidating)

In a current distribution, the partner generally recognizes **no gain** unless the cash received exceeds the partner's outside basis. Property received takes a carryover basis (limited to the partner's remaining outside basis). The partner's basis is reduced by cash and the basis assigned to distributed property.

### Liquidating Distributions

In a liquidating distribution, the partner's basis in the partnership interest is fully allocated to the distributed property. Gain is recognized only if cash exceeds basis. Loss is recognized only if the distribution consists solely of cash, unrealized receivables, and inventory, and the partner's basis exceeds the amount received.

| Feature | Current Distribution | Liquidating Distribution |
|---------|---------------------|-------------------------|
| Gain recognition | Only if cash > outside basis | Only if cash > outside basis |
| Loss recognition | Never | Only if cash + receivables + inventory < basis |
| Property basis to partner | Carryover (limited to remaining basis) | Substituted basis (remaining basis allocated to property) |
| Effect on partnership interest | Reduces but does not eliminate | Terminates the interest |
