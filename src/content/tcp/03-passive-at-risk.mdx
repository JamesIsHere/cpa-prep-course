# Passive Activity and At-Risk Rules

## Learning Objectives

- Define passive activities and apply the seven material participation tests
- Calculate passive activity loss limitations and apply the grouping and suspension rules
- Apply the rental activity rules including the real estate professional exception and the $25,000 allowance
- Determine a taxpayer's at-risk amount and distinguish recourse from nonrecourse financing
- Apply the ordering rules for loss limitations: at-risk, passive activity, and excess business loss

## Passive Activities Defined

A **passive activity** is any trade or business in which the taxpayer does **not materially participate**. Under Section 469, losses from passive activities can only offset passive income — they cannot offset active income (wages, salaries) or portfolio income (interest, dividends, capital gains).

There are two categories of passive activities:

1. **Trade or business activities** in which the taxpayer does not materially participate
2. **Rental activities** — treated as per se passive regardless of participation level (with limited exceptions)

> **Exam tip:** Portfolio income is never passive. Interest, dividends, royalties, and capital gains from portfolio investments are specifically excluded from passive income, even if the taxpayer did nothing to earn them. Do not confuse "passive" in the colloquial sense with the tax definition.

## Material Participation Tests

A taxpayer **materially participates** in an activity if they meet any one of the following seven tests under Reg. 1.469-5T:

| Test | Description |
|------|-------------|
| 1 | More than **500 hours** of participation during the year |
| 2 | Taxpayer's participation constitutes **substantially all** participation by all individuals |
| 3 | More than **100 hours** and not less than any other individual's participation |
| 4 | Activity is a **significant participation activity** (100-500 hours) and aggregate hours in all SPAs exceed 500 |
| 5 | Materially participated in any **5 of the prior 10 tax years** |
| 6 | Activity is a **personal service activity** and taxpayer participated in any **3 prior tax years** |
| 7 | Based on all facts and circumstances, taxpayer participated on a **regular, continuous, and substantial basis** (minimum 100 hours required by regulation) |

**Significant Participation Activities (SPAs):** If a taxpayer participates more than 100 hours but fewer than 500 hours in multiple activities, and the total across all such activities exceeds 500 hours, each SPA is treated as an active (non-passive) activity.

**Worked Example:**

Dr. Chen is a full-time physician who also owns three rental properties and a restaurant. Her participation hours:

| Activity | Hours | Material Participation? |
|----------|-------|------------------------|
| Medical practice | 2,200 | Yes (Test 1: > 500 hours) |
| Restaurant | 450 | No single test met alone |
| Rental #1 | 120 | Per se passive (rental) |
| Rental #2 | 80 | Per se passive (rental) |
| Rental #3 | 60 | Per se passive (rental) |

The restaurant is an SPA (100-500 hours). Since Dr. Chen has only one SPA, her aggregate SPA hours are 450 — less than 500 — so the restaurant is passive.

## Passive Activity Loss (PAL) Limitations

Losses from passive activities are **suspended** and carried forward until:

1. The taxpayer generates **passive income** to offset them
2. The taxpayer makes a **fully taxable disposition** of the entire interest in the activity

Upon a complete, taxable disposition to an unrelated party, all suspended losses from that activity are released and deductible against any type of income.

### Grouping Rules

Taxpayers may **group** activities that constitute an **appropriate economic unit** based on factors including:

- Similarities and differences in types of trades or businesses
- Extent of common control and ownership
- Geographic location
- Interdependencies between activities

Once grouped, the activities are treated as a single activity for material participation and loss limitation purposes. Grouping is generally a one-time election — regrouping is only permitted in limited circumstances.

> **Exam tip:** The disposition must be fully taxable to release suspended losses. A gift does not release suspended losses (they transfer to the donee). A transfer at death allows suspended losses only to the extent they exceed the stepped-up basis.

## Rental Activity Rules

### Per Se Passive Treatment

Rental activities are **per se passive** regardless of the taxpayer's hours of participation. This includes:

- Rental of real estate (apartments, commercial buildings)
- Rental of tangible personal property (equipment leasing)
- Activities where the average customer use period is 7 days or fewer are **not** treated as rental activities (e.g., hotel operations)

### $25,000 Rental Loss Allowance

An individual who **actively participates** in a rental real estate activity may deduct up to **$25,000** of rental losses against nonpassive income. Active participation is a lower standard than material participation — it requires bona fide involvement in management decisions (approving tenants, setting rental terms, approving expenditures).

The $25,000 allowance phases out:

| Modified AGI | Allowance |
|-------------|-----------|
| $100,000 or less | Full $25,000 |
| $100,001 - $150,000 | Reduced by 50% of excess over $100,000 |
| $150,000 or more | $0 (fully phased out) |

**Worked Example:**

A taxpayer with modified AGI of $130,000 actively participates in a rental activity that generates a $20,000 loss.

- Phase-out: ($130,000 - $100,000) x 50% = $15,000 reduction
- Maximum allowance: $25,000 - $15,000 = $10,000
- Deductible rental loss: $10,000 (lesser of $20,000 loss or $10,000 allowance)
- Suspended loss: $10,000 carried forward

### Real Estate Professional Exception

A taxpayer qualifies as a **real estate professional** if:

1. More than 50% of personal services performed during the year are in real property trades or businesses, **and**
2. More than **750 hours** of services in real property trades or businesses

If both tests are met, the taxpayer's rental real estate activities are **not automatically treated as passive**. The taxpayer must still materially participate in each rental activity (or elect to group all rental activities as one).

## At-Risk Rules (Section 465)

The **at-risk rules** limit a taxpayer's deductible losses to the amount the taxpayer has **at risk** — the amount they could actually lose economically.

### Amount At Risk

The at-risk amount includes:

- **Cash contributions** to the activity
- **Adjusted basis of property** contributed
- **Amounts borrowed** for which the taxpayer is **personally liable** (recourse debt)
- **Qualified nonrecourse financing** — for real estate only, nonrecourse debt from a qualified lender (bank, government) secured by the real property

The at-risk amount does **not** include:

- Nonrecourse debt (except qualified nonrecourse financing for real estate)
- Amounts protected by stop-loss agreements, guarantees, or insurance
- Amounts borrowed from persons with an interest in the activity (related-party loans)

| Type of Financing | At-Risk? | Exception |
|------------------|----------|-----------|
| Recourse debt | Yes | None |
| Nonrecourse debt (real estate, qualified lender) | Yes | Qualified nonrecourse financing |
| Nonrecourse debt (non-real estate) | No | None |
| Seller financing (nonrecourse) | No | Unless it meets qualified nonrecourse rules |
| Related-party loans | No | Even if recourse |

**Worked Example:**

A taxpayer invests $50,000 cash and borrows $150,000 on a recourse note to purchase equipment for a business. The business generates a $180,000 loss in Year 1.

- At-risk amount: $50,000 + $150,000 = $200,000
- Deductible loss (at-risk): $180,000 (does not exceed $200,000 at-risk amount)
- Remaining at-risk amount: $200,000 - $180,000 = $20,000

## Ordering Rules for Loss Limitations

When multiple loss limitation rules apply, they must be applied in a specific order. A loss must clear each hurdle before moving to the next:

| Order | Rule | IRC Section | Limitation |
|-------|------|-------------|------------|
| 1 | Basis limitation | Varies | Loss cannot exceed taxpayer's basis in the activity |
| 2 | At-risk limitation | Section 465 | Loss cannot exceed amount at risk |
| 3 | Passive activity limitation | Section 469 | Passive losses offset only passive income |
| 4 | Excess business loss limitation | Section 461(l) | Losses exceeding $313,000 (single) / $626,000 (MFJ) for 2025 become NOL carryforward |

> **Exam tip:** Always apply loss limitations in order: basis first, then at-risk, then passive activity, then excess business loss. A loss that is suspended at one level never reaches the next level. The exam frequently tests whether candidates know the correct sequence.

**Worked Example:**

A partner has a $100,000 loss from a partnership. Her basis in the partnership is $80,000, her at-risk amount is $60,000, and she does not materially participate.

| Rule | Available Loss | Limitation | Deductible | Suspended |
|------|---------------|------------|------------|-----------|
| Basis | $100,000 | $80,000 basis | $80,000 passes through | $20,000 suspended |
| At-risk | $80,000 | $60,000 at-risk | $60,000 passes through | $20,000 suspended |
| Passive activity | $60,000 | $0 passive income | $0 deductible | $60,000 suspended |

Net result: $0 deductible in the current year, with $20,000 suspended at basis, $20,000 suspended at at-risk, and $60,000 suspended as a passive loss.
