# Slayer CPA vs Becker: Full Comparison

If you're studying for the CPA exam, you've probably heard of Becker. It's the most well-known review course in the industry, used by thousands of candidates every year. But at $2,000–$3,500, it's also one of the most expensive.

Slayer CPA takes a different approach: focused self-study materials at $29.99/month. So how do the two actually compare? Here's an honest breakdown.

## Pricing

This is the biggest difference between the two.

**Becker** charges between $2,399 (Advantage) and $3,499 (Concierge) for full access. Some employers reimburse the cost, but many candidates pay out of pocket. There are also payment plans available, though they add interest.

**Slayer CPA** costs $29.99/month with no contracts. You can cancel anytime. If you study for 12 months, that's about $360 total — roughly 10% of what Becker charges.

For candidates on a budget or those who prefer to pay as they go, the pricing difference is significant.

## Content and Format

**Becker** is built around video lectures. Each topic has a recorded lecture (usually 30–90 minutes), a digital textbook section, and practice questions. The Concierge tier adds live online classes and one-on-one academic counseling.

**Slayer CPA** is entirely text-based. Lessons are written explanations with worked examples, key terms, and practice problems. There are no video lectures. After each topic, you take a quiz with detailed answer explanations. There are also timed practice exams and downloadable PDF study frameworks.

If you learn best by watching lectures, Becker is designed for that. If you prefer reading and self-paced practice, Slayer CPA focuses on that approach.

## Question Banks

**Becker** has over 9,000 practice questions across all sections. Their adaptive learning tool (Adapt2U) adjusts question difficulty based on your performance.

**Slayer CPA** has 5,000+ questions across all six CPA sections (AUD, FAR, REG, BAR, ISC, TCP). Questions are organized by topic with detailed explanations. There's no adaptive algorithm — you choose the topics you want to practice.

Becker has a larger question bank, but Slayer CPA's 5,000+ questions provide thorough coverage of every blueprint topic with enough volume for extensive practice without repeating questions.

## Study Frameworks

**Becker** includes final review courses and flashcards as part of their higher-tier packages.

**Slayer CPA** includes downloadable PDF study frameworks for every section. These contain concept maps, decision trees, formula sheets, and mnemonics. They're designed to be printed and used as quick-reference review sheets.

## Coverage

Both courses cover all six sections of the CPA exam and align their content to the current AICPA exam blueprint.

**Becker** has been updating its content for decades and has a large editorial team.

**Slayer CPA** has 96 lessons across all six sections, with content aligned to the 2026 exam blueprint and current IRS figures.

## Who Each Course Is Best For

**Choose Becker if:**
- Your employer is paying for it
- You want video lectures and live classes
- You want the largest possible question bank
- You prefer a structured, scheduled study plan

**Choose Slayer CPA if:**
- You're paying out of pocket and want to keep costs low
- You prefer reading-based study over watching videos
- You want to study at your own pace without a fixed schedule
- You want downloadable study frameworks for quick review

## The Bottom Line

Becker is the industry standard for a reason — it's comprehensive, well-established, and effective. But it's also expensive, and not every candidate needs (or can afford) a $3,000 review course.

Slayer CPA is built for self-study candidates who want solid content, practice questions, and study tools at a fraction of the cost. It's not trying to replace Becker — it's an alternative for people who study differently.

The best way to decide is to try both. Becker offers a free trial, and Slayer CPA lets you preview intro lessons without creating an account.
