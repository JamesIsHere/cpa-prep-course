# SOC Engagements

## Learning Objectives

- Distinguish between SOC 1, SOC 2, and SOC 3 engagement types
- Understand SOC for Cybersecurity and SOC for Supply Chain engagements
- Compare Type I and Type II reports in terms of scope and assurance
- Identify the responsibilities of management vs. the service auditor
- Apply the inclusive and carve-out methods for subservice organizations

## Overview of SOC Engagements

**System and Organization Controls (SOC)** engagements are attestation engagements where a CPA examines and reports on the controls at a **service organization** — any entity that provides services to other organizations (user entities) that are relevant to those organizations' internal controls.

Examples of service organizations include cloud computing providers, payroll processors, data centers, managed IT service providers, and financial transaction processors.

## SOC Engagement Types

| Engagement | Framework | Purpose | Audience |
|---|---|---|---|
| SOC 1 | AT-C 320 (AICPA SOC 1 Guide) | Report on controls relevant to **user entities' internal control over financial reporting (ICFR)** | User entity auditors and management (restricted use) |
| SOC 2 | Trust Services Criteria (TSC) | Report on controls relevant to **security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, and/or privacy** | Management, regulators, prospective customers (restricted use) |
| SOC 3 | Trust Services Criteria (TSC) | Same scope as SOC 2, but with a **simplified, general-use report** | General public — can be freely distributed, posted on websites |

### SOC 1 — Controls Over Financial Reporting

SOC 1 is specifically designed for service organizations whose services affect the financial statements of their user entities. A payroll processor, for example, directly impacts the payroll expense and liability accounts on a user entity's financial statements.

The service auditor evaluates whether the service organization's controls are **suitably designed** (Type I) or **suitably designed and operating effectively** (Type II) to achieve control objectives related to ICFR.

### SOC 2 — Trust Services Criteria

SOC 2 reports evaluate controls against one or more of the five Trust Services Criteria. The **Security** criterion (also called the **common criteria**) is always included. The other four criteria are included based on relevance to the services provided.

SOC 2 reports contain detailed information about control descriptions and test results, making them restricted-use reports.

### SOC 3 — General Use

SOC 3 reports cover the same subject matter as SOC 2 but omit the detailed control descriptions and test results. Because they contain only the auditor's opinion and a general system description, they can be freely distributed.

> **Exam tip:** SOC 1 relates to ICFR, SOC 2 relates to Trust Services Criteria. This is the most fundamental distinction. SOC 2 and SOC 3 use the same criteria — the difference is the level of detail and distribution restrictions. SOC 3 is general use; SOC 2 is restricted use.

## SOC for Cybersecurity and SOC for Supply Chain

Beyond the core SOC 1/2/3 framework, the AICPA has developed two additional SOC engagements:

| Engagement | Purpose | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| SOC for Cybersecurity | Report on an organization's **cybersecurity risk management program** | Entity-wide focus on cybersecurity (not limited to a specific service); uses the AICPA's Description Criteria and Control Criteria |
| SOC for Supply Chain | Report on controls at a **producing or manufacturing organization** relevant to their supply chain | Addresses production and distribution controls; helps customers and business partners evaluate supply chain risk |

SOC for Cybersecurity reports are designed for a broader audience — including boards of directors, analysts, and business partners — who need assurance about an organization's cybersecurity program but do not need the granular detail of a SOC 2 report.

## Type I vs. Type II Reports

This distinction applies to both SOC 1 and SOC 2 engagements.

| Characteristic | Type I | Type II |
|---|---|---|
| What is evaluated | Design of controls **as of** a specific point in time | Design **and operating effectiveness** of controls over a period of time |
| Period covered | A single date (e.g., as of June 30, 2025) | A period (e.g., January 1 – December 31, 2025), typically 6-12 months |
| Testing performed | Inquiry, observation, inspection of control design | All Type I procedures **plus** testing that controls operated effectively throughout the period |
| Level of assurance | Lower — only confirms controls are suitably designed | Higher — confirms controls are designed and actually worked over time |
| Common use case | New service organizations establishing initial SOC reporting | Mature organizations demonstrating ongoing control effectiveness |

> **Exam tip:** Type II provides stronger assurance because it tests operating effectiveness over a period, not just design at a point in time. User entity auditors generally prefer Type II reports. The exam may test whether a Type I report is sufficient for the user entity auditor to rely on — typically it is not, because it does not test operating effectiveness.

## Engagement Planning

### Scope and System Description

The service organization prepares a **system description** that defines the boundaries of the engagement:

- Services provided and the types of user entities served
- Infrastructure, software, people, procedures, and data involved
- System boundaries and the components included in the scope
- Control objectives (SOC 1) or Trust Services Criteria (SOC 2) addressed
- Relevant aspects of the control environment, risk assessment, and monitoring

The service auditor evaluates whether the system description is **fairly presented** — that it accurately and completely describes the system and its controls.

### Control Objectives

For SOC 1, control objectives are specific to the services provided and their impact on ICFR. For example, a payroll processor might have control objectives around:
- Accurate calculation of gross-to-net pay
- Timely processing and transmission of payroll funds
- Proper withholding and remittance of payroll taxes

For SOC 2, control objectives map to the applicable Trust Services Criteria categories.

## Responsibilities

| Responsibility | Management (Service Organization) | Service Auditor |
|---|---|---|
| System description | Prepare and assert that it is fairly presented | Evaluate whether the description is fairly presented |
| Controls | Design, implement, and operate controls | Test and evaluate controls |
| Assertion | Provide a written assertion about the description and controls | Opine on management's assertion |
| Deficiencies | Remediate identified control deficiencies | Report deficiencies and their impact on the opinion |
| Subservice organizations | Define the scope of services and the method for addressing subservice organizations | Evaluate the scope and method |

The service auditor issues an **opinion** on: (1) whether the system description is fairly presented, (2) whether controls are suitably designed, and (3) for Type II, whether controls operated effectively during the period.

## Subservice Organizations

When a service organization outsources part of its services to another entity (a **subservice organization**), there are two methods for addressing this in the SOC report:

### Inclusive Method

The subservice organization's controls are **included** in the scope of the service organization's SOC report. The service auditor tests both the service organization's and the subservice organization's controls.

### Carve-Out Method

The subservice organization's controls are **excluded** from the scope. The report describes the nature of services provided by the subservice organization and identifies the controls that the subservice organization is expected to have in place, but the service auditor does not test them.

| Factor | Inclusive Method | Carve-Out Method |
|---|---|---|
| Subservice organization controls | Tested by service auditor | Not tested — excluded from scope |
| Report coverage | Broader — covers entire service delivery chain | Narrower — covers only the service organization |
| User entity auditor action | Can rely on the single report for both organizations | Must separately evaluate the subservice organization's controls |
| Complexity | More complex and costly to perform | Simpler and more common |

> **Exam tip:** The carve-out method is far more common in practice. When the exam describes a scenario where a user entity auditor receives a SOC report using the carve-out method, the auditor must evaluate whether additional procedures are needed to assess the subservice organization's controls.

## User Entity Controls and Complementary Controls

SOC reports typically identify **complementary user entity controls (CUECs)** — controls that the user entity must implement for the service organization's controls to be fully effective. These are assumptions built into the service organization's control design.

**Examples of CUECs:**
- The user entity restricts who can submit transactions to the service organization
- The user entity reviews output reports and reconciles results
- The user entity maintains proper access controls over its connection to the service organization
- The user entity follows proper procedures for onboarding and offboarding users

The user entity auditor must evaluate whether the user entity has implemented the CUECs identified in the SOC report. If CUECs are not in place, a gap exists in the control environment.

## Practice Problems

**1.** A CPA firm is engaged to report on controls at a cloud hosting provider that are relevant to user entities' financial reporting. The engagement will cover the period from January 1 to December 31, 2025. This engagement is BEST described as a:

a) SOC 2 Type I report
b) SOC 1 Type II report
c) SOC 3 general-use report
d) SOC for Cybersecurity engagement

**Answer:** b) Since the engagement addresses controls relevant to user entities' internal control over financial reporting (ICFR) and covers a period of time (not a single date), this is a SOC 1 Type II report. SOC 2 would apply if the focus were on Trust Services Criteria rather than ICFR.

---

**2.** A service organization uses a third-party data center to host its servers. The SOC 2 report describes the data center's services and identifies the controls the data center is expected to have, but the service auditor did not test the data center's controls. This approach is known as the:

a) Inclusive method — the data center's controls are tested within the report
b) Carve-out method — the data center's controls are excluded from the scope
c) Complementary user entity control — the user entity must test the data center
d) Parallel testing method — the service auditor tests both organizations simultaneously

**Answer:** b) The carve-out method excludes the subservice organization's (data center's) controls from the scope of the SOC report. The report identifies the nature of the services and the expected controls, but the service auditor does not test them. User entity auditors must separately evaluate the subservice organization's controls.

---

**3.** A user entity auditor is reviewing a SOC 1 Type II report and notes that the report identifies several complementary user entity controls (CUECs). The user entity auditor should:

a) Ignore the CUECs because they are the service organization's responsibility
b) Request a new SOC report that does not include CUECs
c) Evaluate whether the user entity has implemented the CUECs identified in the report
d) Conclude that the SOC report is unreliable because controls are shared

**Answer:** c) Complementary user entity controls are controls that the user entity must have in place for the overall control environment to be effective. The user entity auditor is responsible for evaluating whether these controls are implemented and operating at the user entity. Without CUECs in place, there may be gaps in the control environment.
